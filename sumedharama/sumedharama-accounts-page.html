What this means

Running costs: everything spent in a year to keep the monastery going, without new buildings and infrastructure (counted as something the community owns), without wear on buildings (depreciation, not money paid out) and without building-project costs the report counts as spending (see the notes). Residents: the monks, novices and anagārikas living at Sumedharama, on average; lay guests are not counted. Inflation: Portugal's consumer prices index (Eurostat). All amounts in euros, as in the report, as paid at the time.

        5. Notes and all figures

        • Where the figures come from. The community's own accounts spreadsheet for 2012–2024, shared by Ajahn Vajiro: every line of expense for each year and a balance sheet (in euros, years to 31 December). For 2025 the page uses the community's two-page "Report to Abbots 2012–2026". These are not filed annual accounts: they are kept by hand and have no income statement, so a few figures do not add up. Wherever this page uses a different number from the one posted, it is marked with a letter in the table below and explained in the footnotes under it. In 2012 there are no resident counts on the community lists, so the page starts in 2013.
        • Totals. In most years the lines add up exactly to the posted total. Where they do not (2015, 2017, 2025), or where the balance sheet slips, the footnotes say what was used. The posted total is used, and any small difference falls in "Office, fees and the rest".
        • Running costs are the posted total, without three things: wear written off (depreciation, and two write-offs of fixed assets, €4,551 in 2018 and €500 in 2023); building-project costs the report lists as expenses (surveying, architects, engineers, legal costs of the project, shelters, clearing land, tools for the project and the tax on buying land: about €79,000 in 2015 and €1,000 to €7,000 in other years); and interest received (€1,454 in 2025, which the report enters as a negative cost inside its total, so it is added back).
        • Rent. The community paid rent until 2018 (€14,500 to €15,000 in 2012–13, about €6,000 a year in 2014–17, €3,500 in 2018). It is in the running costs, so the first period includes it.
        • Kinds of cost. "Heat, light and water": electricity, water, gas and wood. "Repairs and upkeep": repairs and maintenance of the house. "Insurance and safety": insurance and fire extinguishers (€5,123 in 2016). "Car and travel": the car (fuel, tax, inspection, repairs), travel and stays. "Health, clothes and food": health costs, clothes, food and vet costs. "Office, fees and the rest": financial services, legal fees, bank charges, tools and utensils, office material, publicity, cleaning, communication and anything else.
        • Wear on buildings. From 2023 the report writes off about €105,000 a year for wear on the buildings that came into use (before, €4,000 to €9,000 a year). It is not money paid out, so it is not in the running costs, but it is why total spending in the report jumps in 2023.
        • Money given. The report has no income line. "Money given, worked out" is the net result of the year (the change in the community's funds) plus the year's spending. It includes gifts for building, and for 2013 it uses the corrected 2012 equity (footnote e).
        • Residents are the monks, novices and anagārikas on the community lists (the Winter list in January and the Vassa list in July). Guests, retreatants and lay residents are not counted. Running costs are not divided by the number of residents: the costs also serve guests and visitors.
        • Inflation. Portugal's harmonised consumer prices index (Eurostat, all items), averaged over each calendar year; the energy check uses its "electricity, gas and other fuels" index. "In today's money" means in 2025 prices. All amounts are in euros as given: nothing is converted to pounds.
