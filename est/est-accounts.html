What this means

Running costs: everything the Trust spends in a year, without building work (a one-off, counted as something the Trust owns), without wear on buildings (depreciation, not money paid out), and without a few building costs the accounts count as spending (see the notes). Residents (under each bar): the monks, nuns, novices and anagārikas living at the two monasteries, on average. Inflation: the UK consumer prices index. All amounts as paid at the time.

Sources anyone can check: the Trust's annual reports and accounts on the Charity Commission register (charity 231310) and at Companies House (company 00565499). Residents: the monasteries' community lists. Updated 17 November 2026, 11:48 GMT

    1. The costs in detail

    Fixed and variable costs

    Average a year in each five years. Fixed costs come with having the places: heating and power, insurance and maintenance have to be paid however many people live there or visit. Variable costs follow the people and what they do: welfare and food, and travel. The rest (staff, office, books, fees) is shown apart. The dashed line is the number of residents (right-hand scale).

    Fixed costs: heating and power, insurance, maintenance

    Heat, light, water and council tax; maintenance; insurance; average a year in each five years. The dark line is their total; the dashed line is that total rising with inflation only.

    Each kind of cost against inflation

    Average a year in each five years; the dashed marks are where inflation alone would have taken the first five years. Red where it grew faster than inflation. Each small chart has its own scale.

    Wear on buildings (depreciation), a year

    Each bar is the yearly amount, averaged over the five years. It is the accounts' own figure: every year the Trust's accountants write off part of what the buildings, fittings, equipment and vehicles cost. It is not money paid out, so it is not in the running costs, but it is roughly what would have to be put aside each year to replace them in time.

    How the accounts work it out

    Each thing the Trust owns has its cost spread evenly over the years it is expected to last:

    Buildings1–2% a yearlasting 50 to 100 years
    Site improvements (paths, drains, car parks)10% a year10 years
    Fixtures and fittings20% a year5 years
    Plant and equipment (boilers, heat pumps, solar, machinery)20% a year5 years
    Vehicles20% a year5 years

    For example, a building that cost £1 million and is expected to last 50 years is written down by £20,000 a year. Land is never written down. In the year to March 2026 the wear came to £562k: £264k on the buildings themselves and £298k on fittings, site works, plant, equipment and vehicles, which last only 5 to 10 years and will need replacing long before the buildings do.

      5. Notes and limitations

      • Where the figures come from. Every figure is read from the Trust's annual accounts. The 2021–2025 reports are the Trust's own copies; all others are the scans filed at Companies House, read by eye and checked: each year's parts add up to its totals, and each year's funds follow from the year before. Ten doubtful figures were checked by hand against the scans.
      • Years. The Trust's year runs from 1 April to 31 March and is named by the year it ends in: "2026" is April 2025 to March 2026. The page starts in 2002, the first year with complete resident counts; the accounts go back to 1994.
      • Residents are the monks, nuns, novices and anagārikas on the community lists (the Vassa list in July and the Winter list in January). Guests, retreatants and lay residents are not counted.
      • Inflation. The UK consumer prices index (ONS, CPI), averaged over each April-to-March year. Each dashed inflation line starts at the actual cost in 2002 and rises with the index. The headline figures compare three-year averages (2002–04 against the last three years), so one unusual year can't swing them.
      • Building costs taken out of running costs. In some years the accounts count building costs as ordinary spending. The page takes them out, so running costs are lower than the accounts' total expenditure by these amounts (the year-by-year table below shows the steps from one to the other):
        • Planning fees for the Amaravati Long Term Plan: £53,909 (2013), £192,938 (2014), £14,704 (2015), £3,586 (2016). From 2018 the Trust counted such fees as building work.
        • Interest on the loan for building the Rocana Vihara at Cittaviveka: £11,526 (2006), £38,772 (2007), £33,440 (2008), £20,901 (2009), £1,846 (2010), £669 (2011), £2,337 (2012).
        • Replacing the underfloor heating in the Amaravati Temple, a serious one-off counted inside maintenance: about £175,000 in all, about £7,800 (2007), £157,200 (2008) and £10,000 (2009), as the trustees' reports give it. Maintenance in 2008 was £311,103 in the accounts; the page shows maintenance without the heating work.
      • Welfare and food is one line in the accounts ("Welfare & food"). It includes welfare: the care of ageing monks and nuns, not only food. Since 2023 a fund set aside by the trustees has also paid for the medical and welfare needs of a senior monk at Amaravati and an abbot's one-year sabbatical (£21k in 2023, £15k in 2024, £28k in 2025, £50k in 2026). Without that, welfare and food still rose from £121k in 2024 to £216k in 2026.
      • The steepest rise in running costs came between 2002 and 2011, before the big building programme that began in 2019. Since 2019, after inflation, insurance and welfare and food rose most; maintenance fell slightly.
      • Heat in 2002 was lowered by a council tax refund of £9,761, so rises measured from 2002 alone look larger for that line.
      • Not in the accounts: running costs for each monastery separately, and electricity on its own (it is part of "heat, light, water and council tax").
      • Wear on buildings. Buildings are written down at 1–2% a year (50–100 years); site works at 10%; fittings, plant, equipment and vehicles at 20% (5 years). Land is never written down, and until 1999 buildings were not written down at all.
