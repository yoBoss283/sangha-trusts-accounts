              THE ENGLISH SANGHA TRUST LIMITED
              Report of the Trustees
              for the year ended 31 March 2004
               The trustees, who are also directors of the charity for the purpose
               of the Companies Act, present their annual report                                                    and the audited
               financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2004.
               The charity is a charitable company                                      and was set up 1st May 1956. It
               is governed by a Memorandum and Articles of Association. At the
              Annual General Meeting on 1st November 2003 the company approved
               by Special Resolution some changes to the Memorandum and Articles
               of Association. These changes were registered with the Charity
               Commissioners and approved on the 5th December 2003.
               Objectives of the charity
               To promote and further the teaching of the Buddha by the provision,
              maintenance and support of establishments in the U.K. as residential
               centres for Theravada Bhikkus and such other persons as may be
               designated by the Sangha who have undertaken the rules of training
               and to accommodate, maintain and support aged and necessitous
               followers of the Buddha Sasana.
               Organisation
               A board of directors of up to 6 members, who meet quarterly,
               administers the charity. There are sub-committees covering finance
               and maintenance. The day-to-day management and operation are carried
               out by the secretary in consultation with a representative of the
               monastic communities.
               Directors and trustees
               All directors of the company are also trustees of the charity and
               throughout this report are collectively referred to as the Trustees.
               The new constitution does not allow monastics to be Trustees,
                consequently, before the new constitution was approved, Venerable
                Sumedho Bhikku and Venerable Sucitto Bhikku retired as Trustees on
               4th September 2003 and 6th September 2003 respectively. There can
               be 6 Lay Trustees each holding one share to the value of fl each.
               All of the Trustees served throughout the year.
               Investment policy
                Under the Memorandum and Articles of Association the charity has the
                power to make any investment which the trustees see fit. The
                trustees have considered the most appropriate policy for investing
                funds and have found that investment in short term cash deposits
                meets their requirements in terms of the objectives of the charity.
                occasionally the charity receives donations in the form of shares
                or of property. The policy of the trustees is not to retain these
                investments in the long term.

                Review of activities
               The main responsibility of the Trust today is the upkeep of Amaravati
                and Chittaviveka monasteries. These continue to attract members of the
                public interested to learn or deepen their understanding of Buddhism.
                There are about 50 monastic residents (the Sangha) at the two
                monasteries. The trust is well supported in its task by the generosity
                of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with total income for
                the year 2003/2004 oi £464,054, oi which £32,947 was received
                specifically for the building of the Dhamma Hail at Cittaviveka. The
                generosity of the trust's supporters enabled the trust to meet
                expenditure of £346,406 and to direct a significant part of the
               trust's spending towards the enhancement of facilities at both
                monasteries, reflected in total capital expenditure of £68,493.
                Contingency reserve policy
                The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the finances sub-
                committee and ratified at a trustees' meeting or more often
                should significant changes in circumstances arise. The review
                concluded that in order for the charity to manage its long-term
                commitments, it should maintain its unrestricted free reserves
                at the equivalent of the greater of approximately three months
                typical expenditure, or two years' core essential costs, assuming
                that there is reduced but not nil income.                                           The trustees have
                calculated that on this basis at least £77,250 needs to be retained
                as unrestricted free reserves.
                Risk management
                The trustees have assessed the major risks to which the charity
                is exposed, in particular those relating to the operations and
                finances of the charity and confirm that they have established
                systems to mitigate the significant risks. They have concluded that
                maintaining the unrestriced reserves at the level stated above,
                combined with a regular review of the operational and financial
                systems and controls will provide sufficient resources in the event
                of adverse conditions.

               ACCOUNTING POLICIES
               FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2004
                    Accounting policies
               1.1 Basis of preparation of accounts
                        The financial statements are prepared under the historical
                        cost convention, with the exception of investments which are
                        included at market value and have been prepared in accordance
                        with the Statement oi Recommended Practice, Accounting anc
                        Reporting by Charities (SORP 2000) issued in October 2000,
                       SORP Update Bulletin 1 issued in December 2002, applicable
                        accounting standards and the Companies Act 1985.
               1.2 Incoming resources
                       Voluntary income and donations are included in incoming
                       resources when they are receivable, except when the donors
                       specify that they must be used in future accounting periods
                        or donors' conditions have not been fulfilled; then the income
                       is deferred.
                1.3 Resources expended
                        Resources expended are included in the Statement of Financial
                        Activities on an accruals basis, inclusive of any VAT which
                        cannot be recovered.
               1.4 Tangible fixed assets
                        Individual assets costing £250 or more are capitalised at cost.
                        Freehold land is not depreciated. Depreciation is provided at
                        the following rate to write off all other tangible fixed assets
                        over their expected useful economic lives as follows:
                        Freehold property                                                       1-2 % p.a. straight line
                       Motor vehicles                                                             20 & p.a.
                        Plant, machinery and equipment                                            20 % p.a.
                        Furniture, fixtures and fittings                                          20 % p.a.
                1.5 Property improvements, maintenance and repair
                        Expenditure on projects clearly planned to enhance the capital
                        value of properties is capitalised. All other expenditure on
                        property maintenance and repair is written off as expenditure
                        in the year.
                1.6 Fund-raising costs
                        There were no costs incurred for fund-raising as the charity
                        depended solely on freewill donations.
                1.7 Investments
                        Investments are stated at market value at the balance sheet
                        date. The Statement of Financial Activities includes the net
                        gains and losses arising on revaluations and disposals
                        throughout the year.
                1.8 Expenditure on management and administration
                        Administration expenditure includes all expenditure not
                        directly related to the charitable activity. This includes
                         costs of running office premises, secretarial salary and audit
                        fees.
