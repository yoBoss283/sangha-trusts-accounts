                                Organisational structure and decislon making
                                The company has £12 of share capital comprising 12 shares with a value of £1. Each of the six trustees holds
                                one share, with the remaining six shares held by ordained monks and nuns (Sangha) each holding one share
                                As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the trustees are subject to retirement by rolation on an
                                annual basis. The constitution of the company does not allow Sangha to be trustees. The maximum number of
                                trustees Is six
                                The board of trustees, which meets at least four times a year, administers the charity The trustees have
                                established sub-committees with delegated powers in speclalist areas These include sub committees covering
                               finance and maintenance, together with an advisory group at the Chithurst Buddhist Monastery (Cittaviveka), all
                                of which report to the trustees
                                The day to day management and operation is carned out by the Trust Secretary, Caroline Leinster and Kate
                                Axelrod (a chartered accountant) in consultation with representatives of the monks' and nuns' communities at
                                Amaravatı and Cittaviveka.

                             The charity is closely associated with Hartridge Buddhist Monastery Trust (HartBMT), the Devon Vihara Trust
                             (DVT), the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT) and Harnham Buddhist Monastery Trust (HBMT) All of these are

                                REVIEW OF ACTIVITIES
                                The main financial responsibility of the Trust is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati and
                                Cittaviveka monasteries      These continue to attract members of the public of all faiths who are interested to
                               learn about, or deepen their understanding of Buddhism There are typically about 50 monks and nuns resident
                                at the two monasteries, together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors
                                The monastenes are focal points for an extensive lay community They provide spintual support to, and are
                                generously supported by, members of the Thai, Sri Lankan, Cambodian and Lao communitles, as well as UK-
                                born Buddhists     For many lay Buddhists, being able to support the monastic Sangha - whether materially or
                               practically - is held as great good fortune, in that it helps to generate spiritual blessings or 'ment'
                                The monastenes are open to the public on a daily basis and all are welcome to join in the meditation and puja.
                                Some visitors come to the monasteres and their grounds simply to enjoy their peaceful and calm environments.
                               In particular, at Amaravatı visitors can make use of Amaravatı Wood and also the Buddha Grove At Chithurst
                                visitors can access the 150 acre Hammer Wood, where ecological conservation is being undertaken
                               Meditation classes, open to all members of the public whatever their faith and personal circumstances, are run
                               at the weekend at Amaravati. These classes are free of charge Mindfulness meditation and the cultivation of
                               loving kindness are practices with proven therapeutic benefits, which have been adopted and adapted by mental
                               health professionals.
                                Dunng the year public talks and lectures are given, including the senes of Sunday Talks at Amaravati The
                                Forest Sangha Newsletter is also available on the internet at www fsnewsletter amaravati org The website
                               includes back issues and an index of past articles. Amaravati and Cittaviveka also produce their own
                               newsletters and updates for supporters.
                                The annual Kathina ceremony (where members of the public provıde the Sangha with requisites - including
                                cloth for robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) were held
                               at Amaravati and Chithurst These events are well attended by the supporters and general public
                                During 2012/13 members of the Sangha led 17 meditation retreats at the Amaravati Retreat Centre These
                               retreats ranged in length from 3 day weekends to 5, 6, and 12 days These retreats are very popular and are

                               Reserves Policy
                               The reserves policy is revewed annually by the Finance sub-Committee and more often should significant
                               changes in circumstances anse The sub-committee's recommendatlons are ratified at trustees' meeting
                               The trustees have established a policy whereby the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets
                               should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the greater of (i) approximately six months' typical expenditure, or
                               (iI) two years' core essential costs, assuming that there is reduced but not nil income             The trustees have
                               calculated on this basis that the Trust should aim to have at least £300,000 retained as unrestncted free
                               reserves
                               As at 31 March 2013 free reserves (ie excluding those funds designated or committed to fixed assets) stood at
                               £507,611 (2011-12 £300,695)
                               Due to two generous donations offered in 2012-13 it became possible for the trustees to consider whether to
                               replenish the expendable endowment fund with all or part of the £168,000 allocated from it in 2011-12 It was
                               decided instead to create a designated fund called the Amaravati Development Fund
                               Full details of the charity's reserves are provided in note 15 to the financial statements
