                    The Trustees, who are also directors of the chanty for the purposes of the Companies Act, submit their annual
                    report and the audited financial statements of The English Sangha Trust Limited (the company) for the ended
                    31 March 2009. The Trustees confirm that the annual report and financial statements of the company comply
                    with current statutory requirements, the requirements of the company's governing document and the
                    provisions of the Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP) "Accounting and Reporting by Charities"
                    issued in March 2005.
                    STRUCTURE AND GOVERNANCE
                    Constitution and governing document
                    The company is a charitable company limited by shares and was established on 1st May 1956. It is governed
                    by its Memorandum and Articles of Association, which were last amended, by a special resolution, on 1st
                    November 2003. Legal responsibility for the management and stewardship of the charity is vested in the
                    board of trustees.
                    Method of appointment or election of trustees
                    The management of the company is the responsibility of the Trustees who are elected and co-opted under the
                    terms of the Articles of Association.
                    Induction and training of new trustees
                    As part of their induction programme, all new trustees are required to read and be familiar with a number of
                    key documents, including: the company's Memorandum and Articles of Association, the company's financial
                    statements for prior years and the minutes of board meetings, sub-committee meetings and the Annual
                    General Meeting. They are also provided with information relating to their duties as trustees (CC3). They are
                    required to read, understand and sign the Memorandum of Understanding and Intention.
                    New Trustees are also provided with a Trustees Induction Pack, which includes the documents referred to
                    above, together with additional information about the Charity including: details of procedures at meetings,
                    information about the existing Trustees and shareholders, the Charity's policy on conflicts of interest, an
                    organisational chart and a brief history of the Charity. The Trustees Induction Pack is available in electronic
                    and printed formats.
                    Additionally new trustees are encouraged to make full use of the information available on the Charity
                    Commission website. New and existing trustees can also attend externally run courses.
                    Organisational structure and decision making
                    As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the directors/trustees are subject to retirement by rotation
                    on an annual basis. The constitution of the company does not allow monastics to be trustees. The maximum
                    number of trustees is 6 at any one point in time. There are currently 6 trustees each holding one share to the
                    value of £1 each. All 6 of the trustees named served throughout the year.
                    The board of trustees, which meets at least five times a year, administers the charity. The trustees have
                    established sub-committees with delegated powers in specialist areas. These include sub committees
                    covering finance and maintenance, together with an advisory group at the Chithurst Buddhist Monastery
                    (Cittaviveka), all of which report to the Trustees.
                    The current members of the finance sub committee, which meets quarterly, include:

                   and Chitihurst.
                    Relationship with other organisations
                   The charity is closely associated with the Devon Vihara Trust (DVT) and the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT).
                   Both of these are independent charities whose objectives are consistent with those of the English Sangha
                    Trust.
                   The DVT acts as steward for Hartridge Buddhist Monastery in Devon. The English Sangha Trust owns 50% of
                    the main property at the monastery (the other 50% of the main property being owned by the DVT). On
                    occasions, the charity makes discretionary financial contributions towards the costs of the DVT.
                    The MBT acts as steward for Harnham Buddhist Monastery (Aruna Ratanagiri) in Northumberland. From time
                   to time the charity makes discretionary financial contributions to the MBT.
                    The members of the communities which reside in properties owned by the charity also maintain close
                    association with other Buddhist Monastics in the UK, in Europe, America, Australasia, and Thailand. A full list
                    of these is available from the website www.forestsangha.org. The community is recognised by Theravada
                    Buddhist Communities and Authorities in all countries where Theravada Buddhism has a presence. More
                    distant association is maintained with other Buddhist Associations and communities and other monastic
                    communities.
                    Risk management
                    The trustees have a risk management strategy which includes an annual review of the risks that the charity
                   faces, together with follow up work to monitor progress on managing risk.
                    In accordance with this strategy, during the year the trustees assessed the major risks to which the charity is
                    exposed, in particular those relating to the operations and finances of the charity and confirm that they have
                    established systems to mitigate the significant risks.
                    They have concluded that maintaining the unrestricted reserves at the level stated below (see Financial
                    Review), combined with a regular review of the operational and financial systems and controls will provide
                    sufficient resources in the event of adverse conditions.
                    Related Party Transactions
                    There were no related party transactions during the year.
                   OBJECTIVES AND ACTIVITIES
                    Objectives of the Charity as set out in its Governing Document
                    The principal object of the Charity is to promote and further the teaching of the Buddha in the United Kingdom
                    by the provision, maintenance and support of establishments as residential centres for Theravada Bhikkhus
                    and such other persons as may be designated by the Sangha who have undertaken the Rules of Training and
                   to accommodate, maintain and support aged and necessitous followers of the Buddha Sasana.
                    There have been no changes in the objectives since the last annual report.

                   REVIEW OF ACTIVITIES
                   Details of the charity's main activities for the year are set out below. Details of the charity's objectives for the
                   forthcoming year are set out under the heading of Plans For The Future.
                   The main financial responsibility of the Trust is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati and
                   Cittaviveka monasteries. These continue to attract members of the public of all faiths who are interested to
                   learn about, or deepen their understanding of Buddhism. There are typically about 50 monks and nuns
                    resident at the two monasteries, together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40
                   guests/visitors.
                    The monasteries are focal points for an extensive lay community. They provide spiritual support to and are
                    generously supported by expatriate members of the Thai, Sri Lankan, Cambodian and Lao communities, as
                    well as UK-born Buddhists.            For lay Buddhists, being able to support the monastic Sangha - whether
                    materially or practically - is held as great good fortune, in that it helps to generate spiritual blessings or 'merit.
                    The monasteries are open to the public on a daily basis and all are welcome to join in the meditation and
                    puja.  Some visitors come to the monasteries and their grounds simply to enjoy their peaceful and calm
                    environments. In particular, at Amaravati visitors can make use of Amaravati Wood and also the Buddha
                    Grove. At Cittaviveka visitors can access the 150 acre Hammer Wood, where ecological conservation is
                    being undertaken.
                    Meditation classes, open to all members of the public whatever their faith and personal circumstances, are
                    run at the weekend at Amaravati. These classes are free of charge. Mindfulness meditation and the
                    cultivation of loving kindness are practices with proven therapeutic benefits, which have been adopted and
                    adapted by mental health professionals.
                    During the year public talks and lectures are given, including the series of Sunday Talks at Amaravati. Lay
                    supporters can also receive the Forest Sangha Newsletter, which is published on a quarterly basis, with
                    around 5,000 subscribers. The Newsletter is also available on the internet at www.fsnewsletter.amaravati.org.

                    The annual Kathina ceremony (where lay supporters provide the Sangha with requisites - including cloth for

                    During 2008/09 16 meditation retreats at the Amaravati Retreat Centre were led by members of the monastic
                    community. A range of weekend 5, 6, and 10 retreats were run, attended by a total of around 700 retreatants.
                    Attendance on the retreats is available to all members of the public. No fee is charged for attending a retreat,
                    instead the opportunity is offered to make a donation at the end of the retreat.
                   In addition to retreats led by monks and nuns, a programme of lay supporter led retreats and days of practice
                   open to the public were run by the Amaravati Lay Buddhist Association (ALBA), a lay Buddhist group affiliated
                    to Amaravati Buddhist Monastery.
                    The annual family camp (held each August) has proven to be a successful way of welcoming families into the
                   monastery. This event, which enables families to stay at the monastery for two weeks to participate in
                   activities developed for children and families, was also run by lay Buddhists at Amaravati monastery. Two
                    additional weekend events were also held for families.
                   Members of the Sangha led retreats elsewhere in the UK and overseas, including the US, Spain, Italy, Ireland,
                   Portugal, and Thailand. Members of the Sangha also visit groups to give talks on Buddhism, meditation and
                    Buddhist practice. Additionally Sangha members visit Buddhist prison inmates, carry out hospital visits and
                    attend funerals of supporters
                    The charity supports educational visits from students and during the year a number of groups visited the
                   monasteries at Amaravati and Cittaviveka. Teachers, other religious groups and university students are also
                   regular visitors. The charity also provides material for the study of Buddhism and life at the monastery to
                    these visitors and to others who request them.
                    The books, CDs, tapes and other materials in the Christmas Humphreys Memorial Library are a resource for
                   all the community and can also be used by visitors and long term supporters.
                    During the year the Introduction to Insight Meditation booklet was reprinted. This booklet is freely available to
                    supporters and members of the public.
                    The Amaravati website at www.amaravati.org and the Cittaviveka website www.cittviveka.org provide freely
                    available printed and audio downloads. Both websites have links to Dhamma talks given by various members
                    of the community, which can be accessed at www.dhammatalks.org.uk and www.dhammathreads.org.
                   Volunteers
                    The charity is very grateful for the contribution made by its volunteers, including members of the Sangha, who
                    assist in a wide variety of areas including office and administrative work, maintaining and updating the
                    Amaravati website, maintenance work, gardening, cooking, driving, assisting with ceremonies including
                    Kathina and Vesak (see below) and serving as members of the various committees and advisory groups.

                   FINANCIAL REVIEW
                    Review of financial position
                    The Trust is well supported in its task by the generosity of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with
                   total income for the year 2008/2009 of £896,415.
                    The surplus for the year was £281,188, which enabled the Trust to make further capital repayments of
                    £380,000 (ie in addition to monthly capital instalments) on the loan taken out to purchase Rocana Vihara.
                    The generosity of friends and supporters has enabled a significant part of the Trust's spending to be directed
                   towards the provision, enhancement and maintenance of the facilities at both monasteries.
                    From a Buddhist perspective, the practice of generosity (known in pali as 'dana') provides supporters with the
                    opportunity to benefit from the development of merit.
                    In 2008/09 total maintenance costs, including the repair work on the Temple heating system at Amaravati,
                   were £173,442 (2007/08 - £311,103). The bulk of the work on the repairs took place in the prior year. As at 31
                    March 2009 the total costs expended on the project (ie including professional fees) were approximately
                    £175,000.
                    At Cittaviveka further improvements and repairs were carried out on Rocana Vihara. Ongoing expenditure
                   included the maintenance of the 150 acres of Hammer Wood.
                    Full details of all designated funds are given in note 15 to the financial statements.
                    Similarly full details of the restricted funds are set out in note 15. These funds will be utilised once plans to
                    spend on the projects in the medium term become crystallised.
                    Reserves Policy
                    The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the finance sub-committee and more often should significant
                    changes in circumstances arise. The sub-committee's recommendations are ratified at Trustees' Meeting.
                    The trustees have established a policy whereby the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets
                    should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the greater of (i) approximately six months' typical expenditure,
                    or (ii) two years' core essential costs, assuming that there is reduced but not nil income. The trustees have
                    calculated on this basis that the Trust should aim to have at least £250,000 retained as unrestricted free
                   reserves.

                           ACCOUNTING POLICIES
                           1.1 Basis of preparation of financial statements

                            1.2 Company status
                                  The company is a company limited by shares. The members of the company include the Trustees

                            1.3 Fund accounting
                                  General funds are unrestricted funds which are available for use at the discretion of the Trustees in
                                  furtherance of the general objectives of the company and which have not been designated for other
                                  purposes.
                                  Designated funds comprise unrestricted funds that have been set aside by the Trustees for
                                  particular purposes. The aim and use of each designated fund is set out in the notes to the financial
                                  statements
                                  Restricted funds are funds which are to be used in accordance with specific restrictions imposed by
                                  donors which have been raised by the company for particular purposes. The cost of raising and

                   1.      ACCOUNTING POLICIES (continued)
                           1.5 Incoming resources
                                  All incoming resources are included in the Statement of financial activities when the company is
                                  legally entitled to the income and the amount can be quantified with reasonable accuracy. For

                                 recognised at the time of the donation.
                                  Income tax recoverable in relation to investment income is recognised at the time the investment
                                 income is receivable.
                           1.6 Resources expended
                                  All expenditure is accounted for on an accruals basis and has been included under expense
                                  categories that aggregate all costs for allocation to activities. Where costs cannot be directly
                                  attributed to particular activities they have been allocated on a basis consistent with the use of the
                                 resources.
                                  Fundraising costs are those incurred in seeking voluntary contributions and do not include the costs
                                  of disseminating information in support of the charitable activities. Support costs are those costs
                                 incurred directly in support of expenditure on the objects of the company and include project
                                 management carried out at Headquarters. Governance costs are those incurred in connection with
                                 administration of the company and compliance with constitutional and statutory requirements.
                           1.7 Foreign currencies
                                  Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at
                                 rates of exchange ruling at the balance sheet date.
                                  Transactions in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rate ruling on the date of the
                                  transaction.
                                  Exchange gains and losses are recognised in the Statement of financial activities.
                           1.8 VAT
                                  The charity is not registered for VAT. In common with many other registered charities, The English
                                 Sangha Trust Limited's expenses are inflated by VAT, which cannot be recovered.
