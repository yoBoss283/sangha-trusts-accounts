                 STRUCTURE AND GOVERNANCE
                 Constitution and governing document
                 The charity is a chartable company limited by shares and was established on 1st May 1956 It is governed by
                 ts Memorandum and Articles of Association, which were last amended, by a special resolution, on 1 November
                 2003 Legal responsibility for the management and stewardship of the Charty is vested in the board of
                 trustees
                 The principal object of the company is to promote and further the teaching of the Buddha in the United
                 Kingdom by the provision, maintenance and support of establishments as residential centres for Theravada
                 Bhikkhus and such other persons as may be designated by the Sangha who have undertaken the Rules of
                 Training and to accommodate, maintain and support aged and necessitous followers of the Buddha Sasana
                 There have been no changes in the objectives since the last annual report
                 Organisational structure and decision making
                 As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the directors/trustees are subject to retirement by rotation
                 on an annual basis The constitution of the company does not allow monastics to be trustees The maximum
                 number of trustees is 6 at any one point in time
                 There are currently 6 trustees each holding one share to the value of £1 each 5 of the trustees named served
                 throughout the year, 1 trustee, Sudanta Abeyakoon, was appointed on 17 December 2006

                 established sub-committees with delegated powers in specialist areas These include sub committees covering

                  Review of activities
                  The main financial responsibility of the Trust today is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati
                  and Cittaviveka monasteries These continue to attract members of the public interested to learn or deepen
                  their understanding of Buddhism             There are typically about 50 monastics resident at the two monasteries,
                  together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors
                  The monasteries are focal points for an extensive lay community, with supporters from a variety of ethnic
                  backgrounds The monasteries are open on a daily basıs for medıtation and puja Meditation classes are run at
                  the weekend During the year public talks and lectures are given, including the series of Sunday Talks at
                  Amaravatı Lay supporters can also receive the Forest Sangha Newsletter, which is published on a quarterly
                  basis,    with around 5,000 subscribers                  The Newsletter is also available on the                           internet at
                  www fsnewsletter amaravat org. The website includes back issues and an index of past articles
                  Part of the estate owned by the Trust at Chithurst is a 150 acre woodland with an historic hammer pond and an
                  Iron-age fort This land is managed for ecological diversıty, creating rare heathland, and clearıng encroaching
                  shrubs for the healthy growth of natıve trees The pathways are kept clear and signposted for local walkers and
                  visitors to enjoy this rich environment
                  The annual Kathina ceremony (where lay supporters provide the Sangha with requisites - including cloth for
                  robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) were held at
                  Amaravatı and Cittavıveka
                  During 2006/07 16 meditation retreats at the Amaravati Retreat Centre were led by members of the monastic
                  community A range of weekend 5, 6, 10 and 14 day retreats were run, attended by a total of around 750
                  retreatants
                  In addition to retreats led by monastics, a programme of lay led retreats and days of practice were run by the
                  Amaravatı Lay Buddhist Association (ALBA), a lay Buddhist group affıliated to Amaravatı Buddhist Monastery
                  An annual famıly camp was also run by lay Buddhists at Amaravatı monastery in August and two weekend
                  events were also held
                  Members of the monastic community led retreats elsewhere in the UK and overseas, including the US, Spain,
                  Italy, Ireland, and Thailand Members of the Sangha also visit groups to give talks on Buddhism, meditation
                  and Buddhist practice Sangha members also provide support to other Theravada monasteries and temples in
                  the UK Additionally Sangha members visıt Buddhist prison inmates, carry out hospital visits and attend
                  funerals of supporters

                 FINANCIAL REVIEW
                  Review of financial position
                 The Trust is well supported in its task by the generosity of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with total
                  income for the year 2006/2007 of £870,422
                 As explained above under Achievements and Performance, the most significant development during the year
                 was the part repayment of the loan taken out in January 2006 to finance the purchase of Rocana Vihara
                  The generosity of friends and supporters has enabled a significant part of the Trust's spending to be directed
                  towards the provision, enhancement and maintenance of the facilities at both monasteries
                  In 2006/07 total maintenance costs were £104,110 (2005/06 - £162,812) At Amaravati extensive repair and
                  refurbishment work was carried out on a kutı In addıtion ongoing work took place as part of the general
                  maintenance programme
                  At Cittaviveka improvements and repairs were carried out on Rocana Vihara Ongoing expendıture included the
                  maintenance of the 150 acres of Hammer Wood
                  In 2005/06 the trustees agreed to set aside £100,000 from funds generated into a designated reserve to help
                  finance the repair to the Temple heating system During 2006/07 £7,800 of the funds were expended (on
                  professional fees), leaving a balance of £92,200 These funds are to be used to finance the completion of the
                  Temple heating repairs
                  In addition, at the year end designated funds were held for a number of projects including the designation of
                  £125,000 to finance capital repayments on the mortgage taken out to purchase Rocana Vihara
                  During 2006/07 the Trustees set aside additional designated funds for the purpose of Long Term Welfare,
                  increasing the balance on this fund to £25,000
                  Full details of all designated funds are given in note 15 to the financial statements
                  Reserves Policy
                  The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the finance sub-committee and more often should significant
                  changes in circumstances arise The sub-committee's recommendations are ratified at Trustees' Meeting
                  The trustees have established a policy whereby the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets
                  should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the greater of (I) approximately three months' typical
                  expenditure, or (II) two years' core essential costs, assuming that there is reduced but not nil income The
                  trustees have calculated on this basis that at least £150,000 needs to be retained as unrestricted free reserves
                  As at 31 March 2007, free reserves (ie excluding those funds designated or committed to fixed assets) stood at
                  £200,517 It is anticipated that the free reserves will decrease during the next year
                  Full details of the charity's reserves are provided in note 15 to the financial statements

                  1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
                          1 1 Basis of preparation of financial statements
                                The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention, with the exception
                                of investments which are included at market value, and in accordance with the Financial Reporting
                                Standard for Smaller Entities (effective January 2005) The financial statements have been prepared
                                in accordance with the Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP), "Accounting and Reporting by
                                Charities" published in March 2005, applicable accounting standards and the Companies Act 1985
                          1.2 Company status
                                The company is a company limited by shares The members of the company are the Trustees named
                                on page 1
                          1 3 Fund accounting
                                General funds are unrestricted funds which are available for use at the discretion of the Trustees in
                                furtherance of the general objectives of the company and which have not been designated for other
                                purposes
                                Designated funds comprise unrestricted funds that have been set aside by the Trustees for particular
                                purposes The aim and use of each designated fund is set out in the notes to the financial
                                statements
                                Restricted funds are funds which are to be used in accordance with specific restrictions imposed by
                                donors which have been raised by the company for particular purposes The cost of raising and
                                administering such funds are charged against the specific fund The aim and use of each restricted
                                fund is set out in the notes to the financial statements
                                Investment income, gains and losses are allocated to the appropriate fund
                          1 4 Incoming resources
                                All incoming resources are included in the Statement of Financial Activities when the company is
                                legally entitled to the income and the amount can be quantified with reasonable accuracy For
                                legacies, entitlement is the earlier of the company being notified of an impending distribution or the
                                legacy being recerved
                                Income tax recoverable in relation to donations received under Gift Aid or deeds of covenant is
                                recognised at the time of the donation
                                Income tax recoverable in relation to investment income is recognised at the time the investment
                                income is receivable
