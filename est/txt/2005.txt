                                                                        TRUSTEES' REPORT
                                                                For the year ended 31 March 2005
                 The Trustees, who are also directors of the charity for the purposes of the Companies Act, submit their annual
                report and the financial statements of The English Sangha Trust Limited (the Charity) for the year ended
                31 March 2005. The Trustees confirm that the annual report and financial statements of the Charity comply
                with current statutory requirements, the requirements of the Charity's governing document and the provisions of
                the Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP) "Accounting and Reporting by Charities" issued in October
                2000.
                 Constitution policies and objectives
                 The Charity is registered as a charitable company limited by shares and was set up by a Memorandum of
                Association on 1 May 1956, which were last amended by a special resolution, on 1 November 2003.
                 The principal object of the Charity is to promote and further the teaching of the Buddha in the United Kingdom
                 by the provision, maintenance and support of establishments as residential centres for Theravada Bhikkhus
                 and such other persons as may be designated by the Sangha who have undertaken the Rules of Training and
                 to accommodate, maintain and support aged and necessitous followers of the Buddha Sasana.
                 There have been no changes in the objectives since the last annual report.
                 Structure, Governance and Management
                 Organisational structure and decision making
                 As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the directors/trustees are subject to retirement by rotation
                 on an annual basis. The constitution of the company does not allow monastics to be trustees. The maximum
                 number of trustees is six at any one point in time. There are currently five trustees each holding one share to
                 the value of £1 each. Four of the trustees named served throughout the year and the shareholders appointed
                 one new trustee during the year.
                 The board of trustees, which meets quarterly, administers the charity. There are sub committees covering
                 finance and maintenance, together with an advisory group at the Chithurst Buddhist Monastery (Cittaviveka).
                 The members of the finance sub committee include:
                 Bhikkhu Vajiro                Bhikkhu Jutindharo,
                 Sister Candasiri,             Colin Ash,
                 Martin Evans,                  Keith Hammond,
                 Sharon Hammond                Nancy Wright.
                 The day to day management and operation is carried out by the trust secretary, Keith Hammond (a qualified
                 chartered accountant) in consultation with representatives of the monastic communities.
                 Method of appointment or election of trustees
                 The management of the charity is the responsibility of the trustees who are elected and co-opted under the
                terms of the Memorandum and Articles of Association.

                 Induction and training of new trustees
                 The board of trustees regularly reviews the range of skills required to carry out its duties to identify skills gaps.
                 As part of their recruitment process, prospective new trustees are required to demonstrate the necessary skills
                                      This includes a sound understanding of the Theravadan Buddhist tradition. They are also
                 required to attend trustees' meetings and relevant sub committee meetings prior to being appointed as
                 As part of their induction programme, all new trustees are required to read and be familiar with a number of key
                 documents, including: the company's Memorandum and Articles of Association, the company's financial
                  statements for prior years, the minutes of board meetings, sub-committee meetings and the minutes of the

                 Volunteers
                 The charity is very grateful for the contribution made by its volunteers, including members of the Sangha, who
                 assist in a number of areas including office and administrative work, maintenance work, assisting with
                 ceremonies including Kathina and Vesak (see below) and serving as members of the various committees and
                 advisory groups.
                 Relationships with other organisations
                  The charity has a close association with the Devon Vihara Trust (DVT), which acts as steward for
                 Hartridge Buddhist Monastery in Devon.
                 The charity owns 50% of the main property at the monastery (the other 50% of the main property being owned
                 by the DVT). On occasions, the charity makes discretionary financial contributions towards the costs of the
                 DVT.
                  The charity is also closely associated with the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT), which acts as steward for
                  Harnham Buddhist Monastery (Aruna Ratanagiri) in Northumberland. From time to time the charity makes
                 discretionary financial contributions to the MBT.
                  The members of the communities, which reside in properties owned by the charity also maintain close
                 association with other Buddhist Monastics in the UK, in Europe, America, Australasia, and Thailand. A full list
                 of these is available from the website www.forestsangha.org. The community is recognised by
                  Theravada Buddhist Communities and Authorities in all countries where Theravada Buddhism has a presence.
                 More distant association is maintained with other Buddhist Associations and communities and other monastic
                 Review of activities and future developments
                 The main financial responsibility of the Trust today is the maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati and
                 Cittaviveka monasteries. These continue to attract members of the public interested to learn or deepen their
                 understanding of Buddhism. There are typically about 50 monastics resident at the two monasteries, together
                 with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors.
                 The monasteries are focai points for an extensive lay community. The monasteries are open on a daily basis
                 for meditation and puja. Meditation classes are run at the weekend and during the year public talks and
                 lectures are given, including the series of Sunday Talks at Amaravati. Lay supporters can also receive the
                 Forest Sangha Newsletter, which is published on a quarterly basis, with around 5,000 subscribers.

                  Review of activities and future developments (continued)
                  The annual Kathina ceremony (where lay supporters provide the Sangha with requisites - including cloth for
                 robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) were held at
                 Amaravati and Cittaviveka.
                  During 2004/05 18 meditation retreats at the Amaravati Retreat Centre were led by members of the monastic
                  community. A range of weekend 5, 6 and 10 day retreats were run, attended by a total of around 750
                 participants.
                 In addition to retreats led by monastics, a programme of lay led retreats and days of practice were run by the
                 Amaravati Upasaka and Upasika Association, a lay Buddhist group affiliated to Amaravati Buddhist Monastery.
                 An annual family camp with 90 people was run also run by lay Buddhists at Amaravati monastery in August.
                  Members of the monastic community also ran retreats elsewhere in the UK and overseas, including the US,
                  Spain, Italy, Ireland, Latvia, Sweden, Russia and Thailand. Members of the Sangha also visit groups to give
                 talks on Buddhism, meditation, and Buddhist practice. Additionally Sangha members carry out prison visits,
                  hospital visits and attend funerals.
                  The charity allows educational visits from students and during the year a number of groups visited the
                  monasteries at Amaravati and Cittaviveka. Teachers, other religious groups, and university students are also
                 regular visitors. The charity also provides material for the study of Buddhism and life at the monastery to these
                 visitors and to others who request them.
                  The charity publishes books for free distribution. During 2004/05 the charity published the new book
                  "Intuitive Awareness", by Ajahn Sumedho.
                  The books, tapes and other materials in the Christmas Humphrey's Memorial Library are a resource for all the
                  community and can also be used on occasion, by visitors, guests and and supporters.
                  Future Plans
                  The charity is negotiating the purchase of a property adjacent to Cittaviveka Monastery in West Sussex to
                  provide extra accommodation.
                 There     are            plans to alter the                 current commitment to maintain the monasteries:
                 Amaravati Buddhist Monastery (including the retreat centre), Cittaviveka, and Hartridge.
                 Although SORP 2005 will not be applicable until the financial year 2005-2006, the Trustees are already working
                 towards implementing the reporting requirements.
                  Risk management
                 The Trustees have assessed the major risks to which the Charity is exposed, in particular those related to the
                 operations and finances of the Charity, and confirm that they have established systems to mitigate the
                 significant risks. They have concluded that maintaining the unrestricted reserves at the level stated below (see
                 Financial Review), combined with a regular review of the operational and financial systems and controls will
                  provide sufficient resources in the event of adverse conditions.

                 Investment policy and performance
                  Under the Memorandum and Articles of Association the charity has the power to make any investment, which
                 the trustees see fit. The trustees have considered the most appropriate policy for investing funds and have
                 found that investment in short term cash deposits meets their requirements in terms of the objects of the
                 charity. Occasionally the charity receives donations in the form of shares. The policy of the trustees is not to
                 retain the share investments in the longer term. As part of this policy the charity sold some of its shares during
                  Financial review
                 The Trust is well supported in its task by the generosity of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with total
                 income for the year 2004/2005 of £967,580.
                 The income for the year was significantly higher than in 2003/04. This was due to the receipt of two properties
                 during the year with a combined value of £500,000 (one as a legacy and one as a gift). Both of these
                  properties are being used as monastic accomodation.
                  Donations and income for the year (excluding the two properties mentioned above) amounted to £467,580
                 which was comparable to the £464,054 received in 2003/04.
                 The generosity of friends and supporters has enabled a significant part of the Trust's spending to be directed
                 towards the enhancement and maintenance of the facilities at both monasteries. Of the total income, the sum
                 of £18,150 was received into a restricted fund specifically for the building of the Dhamma Hall at Cittaviveka,
                 which was completed during the year at a cost of £58,605. Another significant capital purchase at Cittaviveka
                 was the installation of a new boiler costing £40,933, which heats most of the site.
                 Funds offered at the Kathina at Amaravati went towards the completion of the kitchen refurbishment, which
                 cost £30,924. Also at Amaravati, work has progressed on the landscaping for the Stupa.
                 In 2004/05 total maintenance costs were £108,498 (2003/04 - £98,791). Work carried out in the year included
                 replacing the brickwork in the cloisters courtyard at Amaravati, repairs to the driveway and paths and ramped
                 wheelchair access to the retreat centre.
                 At Cittaviveka ongoing expenditure included the maintenance of the 150 acres of Hammer Wood.
                  Reserves policy
                 The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the finances sub-committee and ratified at a trustees' meeting, and
                 more often should significant changes in circumstances arise. The trustees have established a policy whereby
                 the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the
                 greater of (i) approximately three months' typical expenditure, or (ii) two years' core essential costs, assuming
                 that there is reduced but not nil income.             The trustees have calculated on this basis that at least £105,000
                 needs to be retained as unrestricted free reserves. As at 31 March 2005, free reserves (i.e. excluding those
                 funds designated or committed to fixed assets) stood at £251,482, which will reduce in the subsequent years
                 due to the projected increase in charitable expenditure.
                 At the year end there was a deficit of £5,433 on the Dhamma Hall restricted fund. This does not take into
                 account a final VAT refund claim (made after the year end), which will reduce the deficit. If any deficit remains
                 it will be covered by a transfer from general funds.
                 Full details of the charity's reserves are provided in note 15 to the financial statements.
