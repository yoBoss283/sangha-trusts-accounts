                  STRUCTURE AND GOVERNANCE
                   Constitution and governing document
                   The company is a chartable company limited by shares and was established on 1st May 1956 It is governed
                   by its Memorandum and Articles of Association, which were last amended, by a special resolution, on 1st
                   November 2003 Legal responsibility for the management and stewardship of the charity is vested in the board
                   of trustees
                   Method of appointment or election of trustees
                   The management of the company is the responsibility of the Trustees who are elected and co-opted under the
                   terms of the Articles of Association
                   Induction and training of new trustees
                   As part of their induction programme, all new trustees are required to read and be famıliar with a number of key
                   documents, including the company's Memorandum and Articles of Association, the company's tinancial
                   statements for prior years and the minutes of board meetings, sub-committee meetings and the Annual
                   General Meeting They are also provided with information relating to their duties as trustees (CC3) They are
                   required to read, understand and sign the Memorandum of Understanding and Intention
                   New Trustees are also provided with a Trustees Induction Pack, which includes the documents referred to
                   above, together with additional information about the Charty including details of procedures at meetings,
                   information about the existing Trustees and shareholders, the Charty's policy on conflicts of interest, an
                   organisational chart and a brief history of the Chanty The Trustees Induction Pack is available in electronic and
                   printed formats
                   Additionally new trustees are encouraged to make full use of the information available on the Charty
                   Commission website New and existing trustees can also attend externally run courses
                   Organisational structure and decision making
                   As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the directors/trustees are subject to retirement by rotation
                   on an annual basis The constitution of the company does not allow monastics to be trustees The maximum
                   number of trustees is 6 at any one point in time There are currently 4 trustees each holding one share to the
                   value of £1 each All 4 of the trustees named served throughout the year, whilst two trustees resigned during
                   the year
                   The board of trustees, which meets at least four times a year, administers the charty The trustees have
                   established sub-commıttees with delegated powers in specialist areas These include sub commıttees covering
                   finance and maintenance, together with an advisory group at the Chithurst Buddhist Monastery (Cittaviveka), all
                   of which report to the Trustees
                   The day to day management and operation is carried out by the Trust Secretary, Keith Hammond (a qualified
                    Chartered Accountant) in consultation with representatives of the monks' and nuns' communities at Amaravati
                   and Chitıhurst

                   Relationship with other organisations
                   The charity is closely associated with the Devon Vihara Trust (DVT), the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT) and the
                   Harnam Buddhist Monastery Trust (HBMT) All of these are independent charties whose objectives are
                   consistent with those of the English Sangha Trust
                   The DVT acts as steward for Hartrıdge Buddhıst Monastery in Devon The English Sangha Trust owns 50% of
                   the main property at the monastery (the other 50% of the main property being owned by the DVT) On
                   occasions, the charity makes discretionary financial contributions towards the costs of the DVT
                   The MBT and HBMT act as steward for Harnham Buddhist Monastery (Aruna Ratanagiri) in Northumberland
                   From time to time the charity makes discretionary financial contributions to the MBT and HBMT
                   The members of the communities which reside in properties owned by the charity also maintain close
                   association with other Theravada Buddhist Communities in the UK, in Europe, America, New Zealand, and
                   Thailand A full list of these is available from the website www forestsangha org The communıty is recognised
                   by Theravada Buddhist Communıties and Authorities in all countries where Theravada Buddhism has a
                   presence More distant association is maintained with other Buddhist Associations and communıties and other
                   monastic communities
                   Risk management

                   In accordance with this strategy, during the year the trustees assessed the major risks to which the charıty is

                   REVIEW OF ACTIVITIES
                   Details of the charıty's main activities for the year are set out below Details of the charity's objectives for the
                   forthcoming year are set out under the heading of Plans For The Future
                   The main financial responsibility of the Trust is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati and
                   Chithurst monasteres         These continue to attract members of the public of all faiths who are interested to learn
                   about, or deepen their understanding of Buddhism There are typically about 50 monks and nuns resıdent at
                   the two monasteries, together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors
                   The monasteries are focal points for an extensive lay community They provide spiritual support to and are
                   generously supported by expatriate members of the Thai, Sri Lankan, Cambodian and Lao communities, as
                   well as UK-born Buddhısts For lay Buddhısts, being able to support the monastic Sangha - whether materially
                   or practically - is held as great good fortune, in that it helps to generate spirtual blessings or 'merit
                   The monasteres are open to the public on a daily basis and all are welcome to join in the medıtation and puja
                   Some visitors come to the monasteries and their grounds simply to enjoy their peaceful and calm
                   environments In particular, at Amaravatı visıtors can make use of Amaravatı Wood and also the Buddha
                   Grove At Chithurst visıtors can access the 150 acre Hammer Wood, where ecological conservation is being
                   undertaken
                   Medıtation classes, open to all members of the public whatever their faith and personal circumstances, are run
                   at the weekend at Amaravatı These classes are free of charge Mindfulness meditation and the cultivation of
                   loving kindness are practices with proven therapeutic benefits, which have been adopted and adapted by
                   mental health professionals
                   During the year public talks and lectures are given, including the series of Sunday Talks at Amaravatı Lay
                   supporters can also receive the Forest Sangha Newsletter, which is published on a quarterly basis, with around
                   5,000 subscribers The Newsletter is also available on the internet at www fsnewsletter amaravati org The
                   website includes back issues and an index of past articles
                   The annual Kathına ceremony (where lay supporters provıde the Sangha with requisıtes - includıng cloth for
                   robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) was held at
                   Amaravati and Chithurst These events are well attended by the supporters and general public
                   During 2009/10 members of the Sangha led 16 meditation retreats at the Amaravatı Retreat Centre These
                   retreats ranged in length from 3 day weekends to 5, 6, and 10 days These retreats are very popular and are
                   usually at capacıty Nearly 700 retreatants participated last year Attendance on the retreats is available to all

                  FINANCIAL REVIEW
                   Review of financial position
                   The charty is well supported in its task by the generosity of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with
                  total income for the year 2009/10 of £1,124,976 which included £500,000 as an expendable endowment fund
                  Full details of all designated funds are given in note 15 to the financial statements Similarly full details of the
                  restricted funds are set out in note 15 These funds will be utlised once project plans are finalised
                  The surplus for the year was £498,929, which enabled the chanty to make a further capital repayment of
                  £20,000 (ie in addition to monthly capital instalments) on the loan taken out to purchase Rocana Vihara)
                   The generosity of friends and supporters has enabled a significant part of the Trust's spending to be directed
                  towards the provision, enhancement and maintenance of the facilities at both monasteries
                  From a Buddhist perspective, the practice of generosity (known in pali as 'dana') provides supporters with the
                   opportunity to benefit from the development of merit
                  In 2009/10 total maintenance costs were £175,074 (2008/09 - £173,442) At Amaravatı work was carried out on
                  repairs to the Retreat Centre roof, extensive electrical wiring in a number of the building, and the construction
                   of a disabled toilet facility
                   At Chithurst further improvements and repairs were carned out on Rocana Vihara Ongoing expenditure
                   included the maintenance of the 150 acres of Hammer Wood
                   As mentioned above under the Review of Performance against Objectives, during the year the charty entered
                   into a contract for the supply and construction of the new Aloka Shrine Room at Chithurst After payment of an
                  intial deposıt, the supplier did not delıver the materials in accordance with the contract The supplier was
                   placed in compulsory liquidation in January 2010                  The chanty sought legal advice in order to recover the
                   deposit but the chanty's lawyers were unable to obtain a refund Accordingly the debt has been written off in full
                   in the accounts

                           ACCOUNTING POLICIES
                           1.1 Basis of preparation of financial statements
                                  The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention, with the
                                  exception of investments which are included at market value, and in accordance with the Financial
                                  Reporting Standard for Smaller Entities (effective April 2008)                 The financial statements have been
                                  prepared in accordance with the Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP)ạẠAccounting and
                                 Reporting by Charties" published in March 2005, applicable accounting standards and the
                                 Companies Act 2006
                           1.2 Tangible fixed assets and depreciation
                                  All assets costing more than £1,000 are capitalised
                                  Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost less depreciation Depreciation is provided at rates
                                 calculated to write off the cost of fixed assets, less their estimated residual value, over their
                                 expected useful lives on the following bases
                                         Freehold property                                  1-2% straight line
                                          Plant and equipment                               20% straight line
                                          Motor Vehicles                                    20% straight line
                                          Furniture and Fixtures                            20% straight line
                           1.3 Company status
                                  The company is a company limited by shares The members of the company include the Trustees
                                  named on page 1
                           1.4 Fund accounting
                                  General funds are unrestricted funds which are available for use at the discretion of the Trustees in
                                  furtherance of the general objectives of the company and which have not been designated for other
                                  purposes
                                  Designated funds comprise unrestricted funds that have been set aside by the Trustees for
                                  particular purposes The aim and use of each designated fund is set out in the notes to the financial
                                  statements
                                  Restricted funds are funds which are to be used in accordance with specific restrictions imposed by
                                  donors which have been raised by the company for particular purposes The cost of raising and
                                  administering such funds are charged against the specific fund The aim and use of each restricted
                                 fund is set out in the notes to the financial statements
                                 Investment income, gains and losses are allocated to the appropriate fund

                          ACCOUNTING POLICIES (continued)
                           1.5 Incoming resources
                                  All incoming resources are included in the Statement of financial activities when the company is
                                 legally entitled to the income and the amount can be quantified with reasonable accuracy For
                                 legacies, entitlement is the earlier of the company being notified of an impending distribution or the
                                 legacy being received
                                  Income tax recoverable in relation to donations received under Gift Aid or deeds of covenant is
                                 recognised at the time of the donation
                                  Income tax recoverable in relation to investment income is recognised at the time the investment
                                  income is receivable
                           1.6 Resources expended
                                  All expenditure is accounted for on an accruals basis and has been included under expense
                                  categories that aggregate all costs for allocation to activities Where costs cannot be directly
                                  attributed to particular activities they have been allocated on a basis consistent with the use of the
                                 resources
                                  Governance costs are those incurred in connection with compliance with constitutional and statutory
                                 requirements
                           1.7 Foreign currencies
                                  Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at rates
                                 of exchange ruling at the balance sheet date
                                  Transactions in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rate ruling on the date of the
                                  transaction
                                  Exchange gains and losses are recognised in the Statement of financial activities
                           1.8 Pensions
                                 The company operates a defined contribution pension scheme and the pension charge represents
                                  the amounts payable by the company to the fund in respect of the year
                           1.9 VAT
                                  The chanty is not registered for VAT In common with many other registered chanties, The English
                                  Sangha Trust Limited's expenses are inflated by VAT, which cannot be recovered
