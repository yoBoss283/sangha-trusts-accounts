                          REVIEW OF ACTIVITIES
                         The main financial responsibility of the Trust is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravatı and
                         Chithurst monasteres       These continue to attract members of the public of all faiths who are interested to learn
                         about, or deepen their understanding of Buddhism             There are typically about 50 monks and nuns resident at
                         the two monasteries, together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors
                         The monasteries are focal points for an extensive lay community They provide spiritual support to and are
                         generously supported by expatriate members of the Thai, Sri Lankan, Cambodian and Lao communıties, as well
                         as UK-born Buddhists For many lay Buddhists, being able to support the monastic Sangha - whether materially
                         or practically - is held as great good fortune, in that it helps to generate spirtual blessings or 'ment'
                         The monasteres are open to the public on a daily basis and all are wefcome to join in the meditation and puja
                         Some visitors come to the monasteres and their grounds simply to enjoy their peaceful and calm environments
                         In particular, at Amaravatı visitors can make use of Amaravati Wood and also the Buddha Grove At Chithurst
                         visitors can access the 150 acre Hammer Wood, where ecological conservation is being undertaken
                         Meditation classes, open to all members of the public whatever their faith and personal circumstances, are run
                         at the weekend at Amaravati These classes are free of charge Mindfulness meditation and the cultivation of
                         loving kindness are practices with proven therapeutic benefits, which have been adopted and adapted by mental
                         health protessionals
                         During the year public talks and lectures are given, including the seres of Sunday Talks at Amaravati The
                         Forest Sangha Newsletter is also available on the internet at www fsnewsletter amaravatı org The website
                         includes back issues and an index of past articles Amaravati and Chithurst also produce their own newsletters
                         and updates for supporters
                         The annual Kathına ceremony (where members of the public provide the Sangha with requisites - including
                         cloth for robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) were held
                         at Amaravati and Chithurst These events are well attended by the supporters and general public
                         During 2011/12 members of the Sangha led 16 meditation retreats at the Amaravatı Retreat Centre These
                         retreats ranged in length from 3 day weekends to 5, 6, and 10 days These retreats are very popular and are
                         usually at capacity Nearly 700 retreatants participated last year Attendance on the retreats is available to all
                         members of the public No fee is charged for attending a retreat, instead the opportunity is offered to make a
                         donation at the end of the retreat
                         In addition to retreats led by monks and nuns, a programme of lay supporter led retreats and days of practice

                          Reserves Policy
                          The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the finance sub-committee and more often should significant
                          changes in circumstances arise The sub-committee's recommendations are ratified at trustees' meetings
                          The trustees have established a policy whereby the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets
                          should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the greater of (i) approximately six months' typical expenditure, or
                          (II) two years' core essential costs, assuming that there is reduced but not nil income The trustees have
                          calculated on this basis that the Trust should aim to have at least £300,000 retained as unrestricted free
                          reserves
                          As at 31 March 2012, free reserves (ie excluding those funds designated or committed to fixed assets) stood at
                          £300,695
                          In accordance with the terms of the expendable endowment fund, the trustees were formally advised in March
                          2011 that should the need anse, part of the fund could be transferred to general reserves in 2011/12 to ensure
                          that the free reserves balance at 31 March 2012 was at least £300,000 Accordingly a transfer from the
                          expendable endowment fund of £168,000 was made during the year For 2012/13 if the need arises a similar
                          transfer can be made from the expendable endowment fund
