                     STRUCTURE AND GOVERNANCE
                     Constitution and Governing Document
                     The charity is a charitable company limited by shares and was established on 1st May 1956. It is governed by its
                     Memorandum and Articles of Association, which were last amended, by a special resolution, on 16th April 2011.
                     Legal responsibility for the management and stewardship of the charity is vested in the board of trustees.
                     Method of appointment or election of trustees
                     The management of the company and the group is the responsibility of the trustees who are elected and co-
                     opted under the terms of the Articles of Association.
                     Induction and training of new trustees
                     As part of their induction programme, all new trustees are required to read and be familiar with a number of key
                     documents, including: the company's Memorandum and Articles of Association, the company's financial
                     statements for prior years and the minutes of board meetings, sub-committee meetings and the Annual General
                     Meeting. They are also provided with information relating to their duties as trustees (CC3). They are required to
                     read, understand and sign the Memorandum of Understanding and Intention.
                     Organisational structure and decision making
                     The company has £12 of share capital comprising 12 shares with a value of £1. Each of the six trustees holds
                     one share, with the remaining six shares held by ordained monks and nuns (Sangha) each holding one share.
                     As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the trustees are subject to retirement by rotation on an
                     annual basis. The constitution of the company does not allow Sangha to be trustees. The maximum number of
                     trustees is six.
                     The board of trustees, which meets at least four times a year, administers the charity. The trustees have
                     established sub-committees with delegated powers in specialist areas. These include sub-committees covering
                     EST-wide finance, plus maintenance at Amaravati, together with an advisory group at the Cittaviveka Buddhist
                     Monastery (Chithurst), all of which report to the trustees
                     The day to day management and operation is carried out by the Trust Secretary, Caroline Leinster, in
                     consultation with representatives of the monks' and nuns' communities at Amaravati and Cittaviveka.
                     During the year, in order to best implement the Amaravati Long Term Plan, a company wholly-owned by The
                     English Sangha Trust and called Amaravati Developments Ltd was set up. This brings together the sangha and
                     professionals in planning and construction, and ensures a proper focus can be given to the work. Amaravati
                     Developments is a non-charitable company with no significant capital assets, number 8766055, and limited by
                     guarantee.

                      Relationship with other organisations
                      The charity is closely associated with Hartridge Buddhist Monastery Trust (HartBMT), the Devon Vihara Trust
                      (DVT), the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT), Harnham Buddhist Monastery Trust (HBMT) and the Milntuim
                      Hermitage Trust (MHT). All of these are independent charities whose objectives are consistent with those of the
                      English Sangha Trust.
                      The HartBMT and the DVT act as stewards for Hartridge Buddhist Monastery in Devon.
                     The HBMT acts as steward for Harnham Buddhist Monastery (Aruna Ratanagiri) in Northumberland.
                      The MHT acts as steward for the Milntuim Hermitage near Crieff, Scotland.
                      The members of the communities which reside in properties owned by the charity also maintain close
                      association with other Theravada Buddhist Communities in the UK, in Europe, North America, New Zealand,
                      Australia and Thailand. A full list of these is available from the website www.forestsangha.org. The community is
                     recognised by Theravada Buddhist Communities and Authorities in all countries where Theravada Buddhism
                      has a presence. More distant association is maintained with other Buddhist Associations and communities and
                     other monastic communities.
                      Risk management
                      The trustees have a risk management strategy which includes an annual review of the risks that the charity
                     faces, together with follow up work to monitor progress on managing risk.
                     In accordance with this strategy, during the year the trustees assessed the major risks to which the charity is
                     exposed, in particular those relating to the operations and finances of the charity and confirm that they have
                     established systems to mitigate the significant risks.
                     They have concluded that maintaining the unrestricted reserves at the level stated below (see Financial
                      Review), combined with a regular review of the operational and financial systems and controls will provide
                     sufficient resources in the event of adverse conditions.
                     The Trust continues to take professional advice where necessary and did so from its auditors and solicitors in
                     setting up Amaravati Developments Ltd, a wholly-owned Company with the responsibility of managing the
                     implementation of the Amaravati Long-Term Plan as instructed by The English Sangha Trust.
                     Related Party Transactions
                     There were no related party transactions during the year.
                     OBJECTIVES AND ACTIVITIES
                     Objectives of the Charity as set out in its Governing Document
                     The principal object of the charity is to promote and further the teaching of the Buddha in the United Kingdom by
                     the provision, maintenance and support of establishments as residential centres for Theravada Bhikkhus and
                     such other persons as may be designated by the Sangha who have undertaken the Rules of Training; and to
                     accommodate, maintain and support aged and necessitous followers of the Buddha Sasana.
                     There have been no changes in the objectives since the last annual report. It was clarified with Bates Wells &
                     Braithwaite solicitors that aged and necessitous followers must be both aged and necessitous, not one or the
                     other.

                     Review of activities
                     The main financial responsibility of the Trust is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati and
                     Cittaviveka monasteries.      These continue to attract members of the public of all faiths who are interested to
                     learn about, or deepen their understanding of Buddhism. There are typically about 50 monks and nuns resident
                     at the two monasteries, together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors.
                     The monasteries are focal points for an extensive lay community. They provide spiritual support to, and are
                     generously supported by, members of the Thai, Sri Lankan, Cambodian and Lao communities, as well as UK-
                     born Buddhists.     For many lay Buddhists, being able to support the monastic Sangha - whether materially or
                     practically - is held as great good fortune, in that it helps to generate spiritual blessings or 'merit'.
                     The monasteries are open to the public on a daily basis and all are welcome to join in the meditation and puja.
                     Some visitors come to the monasteries and their grounds simply to enjoy their peaceful and calm environments
                     In particular, at Amaravati visitors can make use of Amaravati Wood and also the Buddha Grove. At Cittaviveka
                     visitors can access the 150 acre Hammer Wood, where ecological conservation is being undertaken.
                     Meditation classes, open to all members of the public whatever their faith and personal circumstances, are run
                     at the weekend at Amaravati. These classes are free of charge. Mindfulness meditation and the cultivation of
                    loving kindness are practices with proven therapeutic benefits, which have been adopted and adapted by mental
                     health professionals.
                     During the year public talks and lectures are given, including the series of Sunday Talks at Amaravati. The
                     Forest Sangha Newsletter is also available on the internet at www.fsnewsletter.amaravati.org. The website
                     includes back issues and an index of past articles. Amaravati and Cittaviveka also produce their own
                     newsletters and updates for supporters.
                     The annual Kathina ceremony (where members of the public provide the Sangha with requisites - including
                     cloth for robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) were held
                     at Amaravati and Cittaviveka. These events are well attended by the supporters and general public.
                     During 2013/14 members of the Sangha led 16 meditation retreats at the Amaravati Retreat Centre. These
                     retreats ranged in length from 3-day weekends to 5, 6, and 12 days. These retreats are very popular and are
                     usually at capacity. Nearly 700 retreatants participated last year. Attendance on the retreats is available to all
                     members of the public. No fee is charged for attending a retreat, instead the opportunity is offered to make a
                     donation at the end of the retreat.

                     In addition to retreats led by monks and nuns, a programme of lay supporter-led retreats and days of practice
                     open to the public was run. The Amaravati Lay Buddhist Association (ALBA), a lay Buddhist group affiliated to
                     Amaravati Buddhist Monastery ran three retreats and 8 Days of Practice. The Buddhist Womens' Network ran a
                      weekend retreat and 4 Days of Practice.
                     The annual family camp (held each August) has proven to be a successful way of welcoming families into the
                     monastery. This event, which enables families to stay at the monastery for two weeks to participate in activities
                     developed for children and families, was also run by lay Buddhists at Amaravati monastery. Three additional
                     residential events were also held for families.
                     Members of the Sangha led a number of retreats elsewhere in the UK and overseas ranging from weekend long
                     to 10-day retreats. Members of the Sangha also visit groups to give shorter talks on Buddhism, meditation and
                      Buddhist practice. Additionally Sangha members visit Buddhist prison inmates, carry out hospital visits and
                      attend funerals of supporters.
                      The charity supports educational visits from students and during the year a number of groups visited the
                      monasteries at Amaravati and Cittaviveka. Teachers, other religious groups and university students are also
                     regular visitors. The charity also provides material for the study of Buddhism and life at the monastery to these
                     visitors and to others who request them.
                      The Christmas Humphreys Memorial Library continues to be a rare resource of Buddhist books for all the
                     community and can also be used by visitors and long term supporters.
                      During the year the charity continued to receive donations of many thousands of Dhamma books, which are
                     available for free distribution to supporters and members of the public. The Katannuta Group is the main
                     publisher and donor of books for free distribution.
                      The Amaravati website at www.amaravati.org and the Cittaviveka website www.cittviveka.org provide freely
                     available printed and audio downloads. Both websites have links to Dhamma talks given by various members of
                     the community, which can be accessed at forestsanghapublications.org website where Dhamma books and
                     MP3 downloads can also be requested for free distribution.

                    FINANCIAL REVIEW
                     Review of financial position
                     The charity is well supported in its task by the generosity of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with
                    total income for the year 2013-14 of £845,632 (2012-13: £942,681).
                     Full details of all designated funds are given in note 15 to the financial statements. Similarly full details of the
                    restricted funds are set out in note 15. These funds will be utilised once project plans are finalised
                     The generosity of friends and supporters has enabled a significant part of the Trust's spending to be directed
                    towards the provision, enhancement and maintenance of the facilities at both monasteries. From a Buddhist
                     perspective, the practice of generosity (known in the Pali language as 'dana') provides supporters with the
                     opportunity to benefit from the development of merit.
                    In 2013-14 total maintenance costs were £189,435 divided between Amaravati at £130,023 and Cittaviveka,
                     £59,412). (The total 2012-13 figure was £176,548).
                     The maintenance work at both monasteries includes health and safety related improvements, the painting of the
                     wooden buildings at Amaravati and building works such as laying new vinyl flooring in the Retreat Centre men's
                     WCs.
                     Reserves Policy
                     The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the Finance sub-Committee and more often should significant
                     changes in circumstances arise. The sub-committee's recommendations are ratified at trustees' meetings.

                     The trustees have established a policy whereby the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets
                     should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the greater of (i) approximately six months' typical expenditure, or
                     (ii) two years' core essential costs, assuming that there is reduced but not nil income. The trustees calculated
                     for 2013-14 on this basis that the Trust should aim to have at least £300,000 retained as unrestricted free
                     reserves. With the advent of Amaravati Developments Ltd this sum was increased to a minimum of £350,000.
                     As at 31 March 2014 free reserves (ie excluding those funds designated or committed to fixed assets) stood at
                     £451,278 for the company and £446,060 for the group. (2012-13 £463,025 for the company).
                     Full details of the charity's reserves are provided in note 15 to the financial statements.
                     Investment policy
                      Under the Memorandum and Articles of Association the charity has the power to make any investments which
                     the trustees see fit.
                      The trustees have considered the most appropriate policy for investing funds and have found that investment in
                     short term cash deposits meets their requirements in terms of the objects of the charity. Occasionally the
                     charity receives donations in the form of shares. The policy of the trustees is to dispose of such shareholdings
                      as soon as is practical. No donations of shares were received during the year and no sales were made.
                      Going concern
                     After making appropriate enquiries, the trustees have a reasonable expectation that the company has adequate
                     resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. For this reason they continue to adopt
                     the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

                                   statements of the company and its subsidiary undertaking. The results of the subsidiary are

                                   The financial statements consolidate the accounts of The English Sangha Trust Limited and its
                                   subsidiary company - Amaravati Developments Limited. The subsdiary company was incorporated

                                   The company has taken advantage of the exemption contained within 408 of the Companies Act

                                          Freehold property                              1-2% straight line
