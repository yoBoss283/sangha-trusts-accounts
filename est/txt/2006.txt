                                                                For the year ended 31 March 2006
                 The Trustees, who are also directors of the charity for the purposes of the Companies Act, submit their annual
                 report and the financial statements of The English Sangha Trust Limited (the Charity) for the ended 31 March
                 2006. The Trustees confirm that the annual report and financial statements of the Charity comply with current
                 statutory requirements, the requirements of the Charity's governing document and the provisions of the
                 Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP) "Accounting and Reporting by Charities" issued in 2005.
                 STRUCTURE, GOVERNANCE
                 Constitution and Governing Document
                 The charity is a charitable company limited by shares and was established on 1st May 1956. It is governed by
                 its Memorandum and Articles of Association, which were last amended, by a special resolution, on 1 November
                 2003. Legal responsibility for the management and stewardship of the Charity is vested in the board of trustees.
                 Organisational structure and decision making
                 As set out in the Articles of Association, one-third of the directors/trustees are subject to retirement by rotation
                 on an annual basis. The constitution of the company does not allow monastics to be trustees. The maximum
                 number of trustees is 6 at any one point in time. There are currently 5 trustees each holding one share to the
                 value of £1 each. All 5 of the trustees named served throughout the year.
                 The board of trustees,which meets at least five times a year, administers the charity. The trustees have
                 established sub-committees with delegated powers in specialist areas. These include sub committees covering
                 finance and maintenance, together with an advisory group at the Chithurst Buddhist Monastery (Cittaviveka), all
                 of which report to the Trustees.
                 The members of the finance sub committee, which meets quarterly, include:
                 Bhikkhu Vajiro                 Bhikkhu Jutindharo,
                  Sister Candasiri,              Sister Santacitta
                  Colin Ash                     Martin Evans,
                 Keith Hammond,                  Sharon Hammond
                 Nancy Wright.
                 The day to day management and operation is carried out by the Trust Secretary, Keith Hammond (a qualified
                 chartered accountant) in consultation with representatives of the monastic communities.
                  Method of appointment or election of trustees
                  The management of the charity is the responsibility of the trustees who are elected and co-opted under the
                 terms of the Memorandum and Articles of Association.
                 Induction and training of new trustees
                  The board of trustees regularly reviews the range of skills required to carry out its duties to identify skills gaps.
                  As part of their recruitment process, prospective new trustees are required to demonstrate the necessary skills
                  and experience. This includes a sound understanding of the Theravadan Buddhist tradition. They are also
                 required to attend trustees' meetings and relevant sub committee meetings prior to being appointed as
                 trustees.

                  Relationships with other organisations
                  Relationship with other organisations
                 The charity is closely associated with the Devon Vihara Trust (DVT) and the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT). Both
                 of these are independent charities whose objectives are consistent with those of The English Sangha Trust.
                 The DVT acts as steward for Hartridge Buddhist Monastery in Devon. The English Sangha Trust owns 50% of
                 the main property at the monastery (the other 50% of the main property being owned by the DVT). On
                 occasions, the charity makes discretionary financial contributions towards the costs of the DVT.
                 The MBT acts as steward for Harnham Buddhist Monastery (Aruna Ratanagiri) in Northumberland. From time
                 to time the charity makes discretionary financial contributions to the MBT.
                  The members of the communities which reside in properties owned by the charity also maintain close
                 association with other Buddhist Monastics in the UK, in Europe, America, Australasia, and Thailand. A full list of
                 these is available from the website www.forestsangha.org. The community is recognised by Theravada
                  Buddhist Communities and Authorities in all countries where Theravada Buddhism has a presence. More
                  distant association is maintained with other Buddhist Associations and communities and other monastic
                  communities.

                 exposed, in particular those relating to the operations and finances of the charity and confirm that they have
                  established systems to mitigate the significant risks.
                  They have concluded that maintaining the unrestricted reserves at the level stated below (see Financial
                  Review), combined with a regular review of the operational and financial systems and controls will provide
                  sufficient resources in the event of adverse conditions.
                  Related Party Transactions

                 OBJECTIVES AND ACTIVITIES
                 Objects of the Charity as set out in its Governing Document
                 The principal object of the Charity is to promote and further the teaching of the Buddha in the United Kingdom
                 by the provision, maintenance and support of establishments as residential centres for Theravada Bhikkhus
                 and such other persons as may be designated by the Sangha who have undertaken the Rules of Training and
                 to accommodate, maintain and support aged and necessitous followers of the Buddha Sasana.
                 There have been no changes in the objectives since the last annual report.
                  Review of activities
                  The main financial responsibility of the Trust today is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Amaravati
                  and Cittaviveka monasteries. These continue to attract members of the public interested to learn or deepen
                 their understanding of Buddhism. There are typically about 50 monastics resident at the two monasteries,
                 together with around 10 to 12 long term lay residents and up to 40 guests/visitors.
                  The monasteries are focal points for an extensive lay community. The monasteries are open on a daily basis
                 for meditation and puja. Meditation classes are run at the weekend. During the year public talks and lectures
                  are given, including the series of Sunday Talks at Amaravati. Lay supporters can also receive the Forest
                  Sangha Newsletter, which is published on a quarterly basis, with around 5,000 subscribers. The Newsletter is
                  also available on the internet at www.fsnewsletter.amaravati.org. The website includes back issues and an
                 index of past articles.
                  The annual Kathina ceremony (where lay supporters provide the Sangha with requisites - including cloth for
                 robes) and Vesak ceremony (celebrating the birth, enlightenment and death of The Buddha) were held at
                  During 2005/06 16 meditation retreats at the Amaravati Retreat Centre were led by members of the monastic
                  community. A range of weekend 5, 6 and 10 day retreats were run, attended by a total of around 750
                 retreatants.
                  In addition to retreats led by monastics, a programme of lay led retreats and days of practice were run by the             ato un ay hye
                 Amaravati Lay Buddhist Association (ALBA), a lay Buddhist group affiliated to Amaravati Buddhist Monastery.
                  An annual family camp was also run by lay Buddhists at Amaravati monastery in August.
                  Members of the monastic community led retreats elsewhere in the UK and overseas, including the US, Spain,
                  Italy, Ireland, Chile, Portugal, South Africa and Thailand. Members of the Sangha also visit groups to give talks
                  on Buddhism, meditation and Buddhist practice. Additionally Sangha members visit Buddhist prison inmates,
                  carry out hospital visits and attend funerals of supporters
                  The charity allows educational visits from students and during the year a number of groups visited the
                  monasteries at Amaravati and Cittaviveka. Teachers, other religious groups and university students are also
                 regular visitors. The charity also provides material for the study of Buddhism and life at the monastery to these
                  visitors and to others who request them.
                  The books, tapes and other materials in the Christmas Humphrey's Memorial Library are a resource for all the
                  community and can also be used on occasion, by visitors and long term supporters.
                  Dhamma talks given by various members of the community can be accessed at www.dhammatalks.org.uk and
                  www.dhammathreads.org.

                 Volunteers
                The charity is very grateful for the contribution made by its volunteers, including members of the Sangha, who
                assist in a wide variety of areas including office and administrative work, maintenance work, gardening,
                cooking, driving, assisting with ceremonies including Kathina and Vesak (see below) and serving as members
                 of the various committees and advisory groups.
                 ACHIEVEMENTS AND PERFORMANCE
                 Review of Performance against Objectives
                 The trustees identified 4 key strategic planning objectives for 2005/06, which were as follows:
                 - to provide additional accommodation: This objective was achieved with the purchase of a property, known
                 as Rocana Vihara, adjacent to Cittaviveka Monastery in West Sussex. This was in order to provide extra
                 accommodation. Since being acquired in January 2006, the property has been used by the nuns community.
                 The purchase of Rocana Vihara was financed partly from the Charity's brought forward funds and donations
                 received in the year and partly by a loan. Full details of the loan are set out in note 14 to the financial
                 statements.
                - to provide a kuti (meditation hut) in the woods at Amaravati Buddhist Monastery: This objective was
                 successfully achieved, and was very much due to the kind generosity of the Charity's supporters.
                 - to implement and complete the maintenance programme for 2005/06: The programme was completed
                 However the total costs of maintenance work during the year was slightly higher than budgeted. This reflects
                 the scale of the work required. At Amaravati, part of the driveways and parking area were resurfaced and
                 extensive work was carried out on repairing window frames. In addition extensive repairs were carried out on
                 the roof on the south corridor of the Retreat Centre.
                 At Cittaviveka, the repair to the roof of the main house was completed. The property suffered damage from a
                 lightning strike, which necessitated additional repair work. A new fire alarm system was also installed at the
                 monastery.
                 - to increase income from standing orders: This was only partially successful. A decrease in income at the
                 start of the year was partly offset by additional standing orders which were set up mainly towards the end of the
                 year.

                 Financial review
                 The Trust is well supported in its task by the generosity of the Sangha's many friends and supporters, with total
                 income for the year 2005/2006 of £522,349.
                 The generosity of friends and supporters has enabled a significant part of the Trust's spending to be directed
                 towards the provision, enhancement and maintenance of the facilities at both monasteries.
                 The most significant development during the year was the purchase of Rocana Vihara, at a total cost of
                 £775,000. Of this amount £100,000 was financed from brought forward designated funds and donations from
                 the Charity's supporters. The balance of the purchase price of £675,000 was financed by a mortgage loan, full
                 details of which are provided in note 14 to the financial statements.
                 Funds offered at the Kathina at Amaravati went towards the acquisition of Rocana Vihara. The funds offered at
                 the Kathina at Cittaviveka helped to finance the purchase of the new fire alarm.
                 In 2005/06 total maintenance costs were £162,812 (2004/05 - £108,498). Work carried out in the year included
                 repairs to driveways and parking areas at Amaravati and repairs to the Retreat Centre roof.
                 At Cittaviveka ongoing expenditure included the maintenance of the 150 acres of Hammer Wood.
                  This year the trustees have agreed to set aside £100,000 from tunds generated into a designated reserve to
                 help finance the repair to the Temple Heating. In addition at the year end designated funds were held for a
                 number of projects including: the Rocana Vihara Fund, which is to finance the loan payments on the mortgage
                 taken out to purchase Rocana Vihara and the costs of the initial maintenance programme, and the Kuti Fund
                 which is cover the cost of constructing a second kuti in the woods at Amaravati. Full details of all designated
                 funds are given in note 16 to the financial statements.
                  Reserves policy
                  The reserves policy is reviewed annually by the finance sub-committee and more often should significant
                  changes in circumstances arise. The sub-committee's recommendations are ratified at Trustees' Meeting.
                  The trustees have established a policy whereby the unrestricted funds not committed to tangible fixed assets
                 should be sufficient to cover the equivalent of the greater of (i) approximately three months' typical expenditure,
                  or (ii) two years' core essential costs, assuming that there is reduced but not nil income. The trustees have
                  calculated on this basis that at least £150,000 needs to be retained as unrestricted free reserves.
                  As at 31 March 2006, free reserves (i.e. excluding those funds designated or committed to fixed assets) stood
                  at £179,173. It is anticipated that the free reserves will decrease during the next year.
                  Full details of the charity's reserves are provided in note 16 to the financial statements

                 Investment policy and performance
                 Under the Memorandum and Articles of Association the charity has the power to make any investments which
                 the trustees see fit.
                 The trustees have considered the most appropriate policy for investing funds and have found that investment in
                 short term cash deposits meets their requirements in terms of the objects of the charity. Occasionally the
                 charity receives donations in the form of shares. The policy of the trustees is not to retain the share
                 investments in the longer term. As part of this policy the charity sold most of its share investments during the
                 year, realising a gain on disposal of £1,491. The balance of its portfolio is represented by shares in two funds
                 which are in administration. Consequently these have been written down to Ehil.
                 Plans for the future
                 The Trustees have identified the following objectives for the year to 31 March 2007.
                  - commence the repair work on the Temple Heating
                  - seek to repay part of the capital on the mortgage loan
                  - implement the initial maintenance work on Rocana Vihara
                  - construct a new Kuti at ABM
                  - launch the new ABM website

                          1.3 Fund accounting
                                General funds are unrestricted funds which are available for use at the discretion of the Trustees in
                                furtherance of the general objectives of the Charity and which have not been designated for other
                                purposes.
                                Designated funds comprise unrestricted funds that have been set aside by the Trustees for
                                particular purposes. The aim and use of each designated fund is set out in the notes to the financial
                                statements.
                                Restricted funds are funds which are to be used in accordance with specific restrictions imposed by
                                donors which have been raised by the Charity for particular purposes. The cost of raising and
                                administering such funds are charged against the specific fund. The aim and use of each restricted
                                fund is set out in the notes to the financial statements.
                                Investment income, gains and losses are allocated to the appropriate fund.
                          1.4 Incoming resources
                                All incoming resources are included in the Statement of Financial Activities when the Charity is
                                legally entitled to the income and the amount can be quantified with reasonable accuracy. For
                                legacies, entitlement is the earlier of the Charity being notified of an impending distribution or the
                                 legacy being received.
                                Income tax recoverable in relation to donations received under Gift Aid or deeds of covenant is
                                recognised at the time of the donation.
