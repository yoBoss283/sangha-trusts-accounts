## Points

1. **Cards put £949,000, £138,000 and £27,000 side by side with one "A day for each resident" line (£41, £55, £10).**
   A reader will rank the places by it. £10 a day for one resident at Hartridge only makes sense if the monks live on
   very little and much is given in kind; the accounts do not show gifts in kind. The small print says "not a ranking",
   but the card itself invites one. Suggest: keep the per-day figure for each trust's own history, and drop the
   per-resident line from the cards (owner already dropped it on the EST page as "not reliable"; same reason here).

   **Reply (landing session): disagree for now, owner decision.** The owner asked for this line on 17 Nov in this session ("a day for each resident, with the number of residents in brackets, both numbers in pounds"), after the earlier "no figures per person" rule. I kept it and made the small print say what it is (a simple guide; guests and visitors use the place too). Not done: dropping it. If you still think it invites a ranking, put it to the owner in their session; I will remove it on their word. Your Hartridge point is fair (£10 a day; food is given, and the accounts do not show gifts in kind): the folded small print now says "not what one person costs".

2. **Per resident hides the Ratanagiri story.** Residents fell from 9.5 (2023) to 4.5 (2025) while costs stayed
   £125–151k. The card says "7 residents", a three-year average that hides a community half the size. If a per-resident
   figure stays, show the latest year or the fall.

   **Reply: agree, done.** Each card now shows the latest year next to the average: "(63 residents on average; 2026: 59.5)", "(7 residents on average; 2025: 4.5)", "(7 residents on average; 2025: 6.5)". Not done: a sentence on the Ratanagiri fall (9.5 in 2023 to 4.5 in 2025) on the card; that is a message change for the owner (card sentences are "later").

3. **"As paid" figure first, after-inflation claim second, on every card.** The big number is as paid; the sentence below
   is after inflation. A reader mixes them. Either put both in 2026 pounds, or add "(as paid)" next to the big number.

   **Reply: agree, done.** The big figure now reads "a year to run, as paid, 2024–26 average". The sentences below say "after inflation", so the two are labelled apart. Not done: putting both in one year's prices (owner decision).
