What this means

Running costs: everything the Trust spends in a year to keep the monastery going, without new buildings and infrastructure (counted as something the Trust owns) and without building work the accounts count as spending (see the notes). Residents: the monks, novices and anagārikas living at Hartridge, on average; lay guests are not counted. Inflation: the UK consumer prices index. All amounts as paid at the time, unless the page says "in today's money".

      4. Notes and all figures

      • Where the figures come from. Every figure is read from the Trust's annual accounts as filed at Companies House (all scans), by eye from the page: each year's total agrees with the next year's report, which repeats it, and from 2019 the eight cost lines add up to the total.
      • Years. The Trust's year runs from January to December. The page starts in 2013, the first full year. The Hartridge Buddhist Monastery Trust was set up in September 2010 as the successor of the Devon Vihara Trust, and took over the monastery's land (£600,000) in 2012. Its first set of accounts, for 15 months to December 2012, shows only £3,767 of spending, so that period is not shown.
      • Running costs are the total spending in the accounts, without building work counted as spending: £19,912 in 2013 and £32,880 in 2014 for the Dhamma Hall project, paid from the building fund. From 2015 the accounts add such costs to the buildings instead. Everything else is in, including welfare, the car and travel, and small purchases of equipment.
      • Cost lines before 2019. The copies of the 2012 to 2018 accounts at Companies House stop before the detailed list of costs, so for those years only the yearly total is known.
      • Kinds of cost. "Rates and utilities" and "estate expenses" (the grounds and woodland, repairs and the log boiler) come with having the place. "Welfare" is the care of the resident monks (the trustees also hold a Long-Term Welfare Fund for the care of elderly or ill monks). "Motor and travel" is the car and the monks' travel. 2023 includes £7,099 for a monk's visit to New Zealand, paid for by a donor. The rest is office work (administration, telephone and internet, the accountant's fee) and small equipment.
      • Not in the accounts. Food is not a cost line: it is given by supporters. The monastery's heating comes from a log boiler; the Renewable Heat Incentive payments it brings in (£1,600 to £2,100 a year) are income, not a reduction in costs.
      • Residents are the monks, novices and anagārikas on the community lists (the Winter list in January and the Vassa list in July). Guests, retreatants and lay residents are not counted. The trustees' reports say two to four lay guests stay at any one time. Running costs are not divided by the number of residents: the costs also serve guests and visitors.
      • Money for building and for care. In 2025 a gift of £222,222 for an Ageing Samanas Fund was received. It is money set aside for improved facilities for ageing monks, not spending, so it is not in the running costs. The cottage plan (Odle Cottage) cost £11,713 in 2025 for planning work and is added to the buildings.
      • Inflation. The UK consumer prices index (ONS, CPI), averaged over each calendar year. "In today's money" means in 2025 prices.
