        Objectives and Aims
                      The Objectives of the Charity are to promote and further the teaching of the Buddha (Buddha Sasana) by:
                       providing, maintaining and supporting, in accordance with the principles of the Theravada Vinaya,
                     residential establishments for a monastic sangha of the persons who have undertaken the Rules of training
                     (Sikkhapada)
                       Supporting lay people in the practice and teachings of the Buddha; and
                       Such other means as may from time to time be decided
        Review of Activities
                      The Trust is the succession Trust of the Devon Vihara Trust (Charity no. 289636)
                      The main financial responsibility of the trust is the development, maintenance and upkeep of Hartridge
                      Buddhist monastery. This continues to attract members of the public of all faiths who are interested to learn
                      about, or deepen their understanding of Buddhism and are able to visit or stay at the monastery.
                      The monastery is the focal point for an extensive community. It provides spiritual support to and is generously
                      supported by expatriate members of the Thai, Sri Lankan, Cambodian and Lao communities, as well as UK-
                      born Buddhists. The annual Kathina and Vesak ceremonies held at the monastery both attract 100-200
                      Visitors
                      The monasteries are open to the public on a daily basis and all are welcome to join in the formal meditation
                      and puja activities. Some visitors request to stay for an extended period in order to deepen their practice of
                     the Buddhist religion. Others gain benefit from the peaceful and calm woodland environment where ecological
                      conservation is being undertaken through the contribution of volunteers.
                      Meditation classes, which are open to all members of the public whatever their faith and personal
                      circumstances, are run on a regular basis. These classes are provided free of charge. Mindfulness
                      meditation and the cultivation of loving kindness are practices with proven therapeutic benefits which have
                      been adopted and adapted by healthcare professionals
                      Meditation classes and residential retreats at other locations, as well as public talks and lectures, are given
                      during the year, and these include visits to schools and other organisations.

         OBJECTIVES AND ACTIVITIES
         ACHIEVEMENT AND PERFORMANCE
                     The resident community numbered six to nine, which meant that the monastery was full much of the time. As
                     usual, there would generally be two to four lay guests at any one time.
                     Meditation workshops every three weeks were well attended. Festival days were well attended, including
                     another large and successful Kathina festival in November held in Chard. Several retreats were taught
                     elsewhere (including in South Africa and the USA as well as in the UK) and a Thai retreats was held at the
                     monastery. There were inter-monastery visits and exchanges, including visits to our sister monasteries in Italy
                     and Massachusetts, USA.
                     Total donations during the year were £337,782. The stand-out item here was a donation of £222,222 specifically
                     to an Ageing Samanas Fund. This is intended to provide better facilities at the monastery for the ageing
                     monastics. Such improvements are already a part of the Odle Cottage project, which is still at a formative stage.
                     Meanwhile, the Trust spent £27,417 on the day-to-day running expenditure of Hartridge, of which £978 was on
                     administration for the independent examiners fee and £1,800 on sundry capital items.
                     Unrestricted income was £113,760 of which £8,561 was Gift Aid from HMRC, £10,029 was bank deposit
                     interest, and £1,865 were payments under the Renewable Heat Incentive scheme for heat generated by the log
                     boiler. There was no Welfare fund income or expenditure in the year. The Long-Term Welfare Fund, specifically
                     for care costs for elderly or ill resident monks, received a further donation of £1,800 in January 2025 to bring the
                     total Fund to £4,300. And as noted above there was a new donation for an Ageing Samanas Fund of £222,222
                     The Trust carried out further work on the Odle Cottage project, putting together a planning application with
                     Group Emmett Design Architects and other consultants, spending £11,713 in the year. However, because of the
                     high cost of the Biodiversity Net Gain requirement of the planning application, it was decided to defer the
                     application pending publication of the government's review of BNG. At year end the government had indicated
                     that "minor" projects such as ours would be exempt from the BNG requirement, saving the Trust potentially £20-
                     30,000 on the project, but the Trust was awaitng publication of the details of the exemption before proceeding
                    with the planning application. The Trust's excess funds continue to be designated for a future Cottage renewal
                     project.
                     There were discussions with a neighbour about their potential sale to us of a piece of adjacent woodland. Having
                     taken advice from a land agent, the Trust made an offer for the woodland, but the neighbour eventually decided
                    against a sale at this time.
         FINANCIAL REVIEW
         Reserves and Investment Policy
                     The Trust holds a contingency reserve of £15,000, primarily to cover any sudden and major fall in voluntary
                    income. This sum represents approximately one year of core expenditure, so it is thought that if a situation arose
                    where either short or long term income seemed in doubt, the reserve would provide a,period of between one to
                    three years for the trustees to consider how best to proceed. The reserve was also available as a contingency
                    against cost over-runs during the Dhamma Hall Project and will be a useful contingency for the proposed Odle
                     Cottage replacement project.
                     The remainder of the Trusts unrestricted cash assets, over and above the reserve, are designated for the Odle
                     Cottage replacement project. Most of these funds are held in interest bearing bank deposit accounts. The Trust
                   • also holds current accounts for immediate running costs.

                              All incoming resources are included on the Statement of Financial Activities when
                              the charity is legally entitled to the income and the amount can be quantified with
                              reasonable accuracy.
                              Resources Expended
                              Expenditure is accounted for on an accruals basis and has been classified under
                              headings that aggregate all cost related to the category. Where costs cannot'be
                              directly attributed to particular headings they have been allocated to activities on a
                              basis consistent with the use of resources.
                              Tangible Fixed Assets
                              Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off assets over its
                              estimated useful life.
                              - Land and Property  - 0% on cost
