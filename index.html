Accounts of the monasteries, from their published annual reports

What it costs to run the monasteries

Eighteen monasteries in Europe, North America, Australia and New Zealand (Amaravati and Chithurst are one trust). Each report says, in plain words, what the place costs to run and how that has changed after inflation.

How to read these side by side

Not a ranking: the places are different sizes and the amounts are as paid at the time, averaged over each place's last three years.

The four places with full reports (the costs line by line) on one measure: running costs after inflation, the three years 2019–21 against each place's last three years. The English Sangha Trust +19%, Aruna Ratanagiri +8%, Hartridge −4%, Sumedharama +1% (compared in its own euros and Portuguese prices). The places differ in size, in how many people live there and in building work, so this shows how each changed, not which is better run.

More about the figures

Amaravati and Chithurst are two monasteries run together: the accounts do not give a figure for each. A day (or a year) for each resident is the cost of a day (or a year) divided by the number of residents (the monks, nuns, novices and anagārikas living there). It is a simple guide: guests, retreatants and visitors use the place too, so it is not what one person costs.

Running costs means everything the trust spends in a year to keep the place going, without new buildings and without wear on buildings (depreciation, which is not money paid out). Each report also takes out a few building costs the accounts count as spending; the notes in each report list them. The English Sangha Trust's own page quotes its latest year (£2,770 a day in the year to March 2026).

Years. The English Sangha Trust's year runs from April to March (2024–26 is the years to March 2024, 2025 and 2026). The others run January to December. Residents are averaged over the same years.
