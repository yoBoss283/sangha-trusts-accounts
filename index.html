Accounts of the monasteries, from their published annual reports

What it costs to run the monasteries

Eighteen monasteries in Europe, North America, Australia and New Zealand (Amaravati and Chithurst are one trust). Each report says, in plain words, what the place spends and, where its accounts allow it, how that has changed after inflation.

Two kinds of number. A dark Running cost label means building work and wear on buildings have been taken out, so these figures can be compared with each other (roughly, as the currencies differ). A dashed Total spent label means the place publishes only its total spending, which can include building work; it is not a running cost, so please do not compare it with the others.

Within each group, places that show their running costs (building work and wear on buildings taken out) come first, from the highest a year to the lowest (the last three years, as paid). Places that publish only totals (money in and total spending, for some what they hold) show those totals, marked "Total spent": their spending can include building work and wear on buildings, so no running cost, day or per-person figure is given for them. Amounts are in each country's own currency and are not converted (Lokuttara is the one exception: euros, with the Norwegian kroner in brackets), so compare them only roughly across currencies.

How to read these side by side

Not a ranking: the places are different sizes and the amounts are as paid at the time, averaged over each place's last three years.

The four places with full reports (the costs line by line) on one measure: running costs after inflation, the three years 2019–21 against each place's last three years. The English Sangha Trust +19%, Aruna Ratanagiri +8%, Hartridge −4%, Sumedharama +1% (compared in its own euros and Portuguese prices). The places differ in size, in how many people live there and in building work, so this shows how each changed, not which is better run.

More about the figures

Amaravati and Chithurst are two monasteries run together: the accounts do not give a figure for each. A day (or a year) for each resident is the cost of a day (or a year) divided by the number of residents (the monks, nuns, novices and anagārikas living there). It is a simple guide: guests, retreatants and visitors use the place too, so it is not what one person costs.

Running costs means everything the trust spends in a year to keep the place going, without new buildings and without wear on buildings (depreciation, which is not money paid out). Each report also takes out a few building costs the accounts count as spending; the notes in each report list them. The English Sangha Trust's own page quotes its latest year (£2,770 a day in the year to March 2026).

Years. The English Sangha Trust's year runs from April to March (2024–26 is the years to March 2024, 2025 and 2026). The others run January to December. Residents are averaged over the same years.

No public accounts found

Monasteries on the community lists whose accounts I could not find published. Where the legal body is known, it is named, so anyone can ask it directly.

  • Santacittarama (Italy): Associazione Santacittarama, recognised as a religious body in 1995 and a member of the Unione Buddhista Italiana. Its website shows no accounts.
  • Dhammapala (Switzerland): the supporting association, Verein Dhammapala (Bern commercial register, CHE-109.752.928). No accounts published.
  • Suddhavari (Brazil): donations go to the Sociedade Budista do Brasil (CNPJ 34.021.832/0001-06). No accounts for the monastery found.
  • Pannagiri (Spain): its steward is named in a UK charity's report as the Comunidad Budista Bosque Theravada. No accounts found.
  • Vimutti (New Zealand): the legal body was not found.
