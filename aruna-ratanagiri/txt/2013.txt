                FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2013
                The trustees present their report and accounts for the year ended 31 December 2013.
                The accounts have been prepared in accordance with the accounting policies set out in note 1 to the accounts
                and comply with the overning document, the Companies Act 2006 and the Statement of Recommended
                Practice, "Accounting and Reporting by Charities", issued in March 2005.
                Structure, governance and management
                The trust is a company limited by guarantee.
                The trustees, who are also the directors for the purpose of company law, and who served during the year
                were:
                Mrs KA Jones
                Ms P M Padbury
                Dr M Pearce
                Daw Myint Su
                Mr J T Bower

                The members of the charity may by ordinary resolution at an annual or extraordinary general meeting appoint
                and remove directors.
                None of the trustees has any beneficial interest in the company. All of the trustees are members of the
                company and guarantee to contribute £1 in the event of a winding up.
                Details of organisational structure - see appendix 1.
                The Harham Buddhist Monastery Trust will succeed the Magga Bhavaka Trust in running the monastery.
                The trustees have assessed the major risks to which the trust is exposed, and are satisfied that systems are in
                place to mitigate exposure to the major risks.
               Objectives and activities
                The objects are to promote the teachings of the Buddha (Buddha Sasana) by:
                a) Providing, maintaining and supporting in accordance with the principles of the Theravada Vinaya,
                residential establishments for a monastic sangha of persons who have undertaken the rules of training
                (Sikkhapada).
                b) Supporting lay people in the practise of the teachings of the Buddha.
                c) Such other means as may from time to time be decided.
                In addition the trust supports the aged and the necessitous/and the doing of all such other things as are
                conducive to the attainment of that object.
                The policies adopted in furtherance of these objects are detailed in appendix 1.
                The trustees have paid due regard to guidance issued by the Charity Commission in deciding what activities
                the trust should undertake.
                Details of the activities of the charity during the year are included in appendix 1.

                1 Accounting policies
                1.1 Basis of preparation
                       The accounts have been prepared under the historical cost convention except for the valuation of
                       investment assets, which are shown at market value .
                       The accounts have been prepared in accordance with applicable accounting standards, the Statement of
                       Recommended Practice, "Accounting and Reporting by Charities", issued in March 2005 and the
                       Companies Act 2006.
                1.2 Incoming resources
                       Incoming resources are accounted for on a receivable basis in the period in which the company is entitled
                       to receipt and the amount can be measured with reasonable certainty. Income is deferred only when the
                       charity has to fulfil conditions before becoming entitled to it or where the donor specifies that it be
                       expended in future periods.
                1.3   Resources expended
                       The policy for including items within the relevant activity categories of resources expended is on an
                       accruals basis. Expenditure is recognised when a liability is incurred.
                       In particular the policy for including items within costs of generating funds, charitable activities and
                       governance costs is:
                       Charitable activities include expenditure associated with promoting the teachings of the Buddha and
                       include both the direct costs and support costs relating to this activity.
                       Governance costs include those costs incurred in the governance of the charity and its assets and are
                       primarily associated with the constitutional and statutory requirements.
                       Support costs have been split between charitable and governance costs.
               1.4  Tangible fixed assets and depreciation
                       Tangible fixed assets other than freehold land are stated at cost less depreciation. Depreciation is
                       provided at rates calculated to write off the cost less estimated residual value of each asset over its
                       expected useful life, as follows:
                       Freehold land is not depreciated
                       Freehold buildings                                     1% Straight Line
                       Solar panels                                          4% Straight Line
                       Other fixtures, fittings & equipment                   25% Straight Line
               1.5   Leasing and hire purchase commitments
                      Rentals payable in respect of operating leases where substantially all the benefits and risks of ownership
                       remain with the lessor are charged to the Statement of Financial Activities as incurred.
               1.6   Investments
                       Current asset investments are stated at market value.
                1.7 Investment Income
                       Bank interest is included in the income and expenditure account on a receivable basis.

                THE TRUST
                    Harnham Buddhist Monastery Trust (HBMT) is the governing body responsible for the stewardship of Harnham
                 Buddhist Monastery. In 2013 the lease to 2 Harnham Hall Cottages, which houses the main monastery reception room
                and kitchen, was transferred to HBMT from the Magga Bhavaka Trust (MBT), the charitable trust which HBMT has now
                completely replaced. The final meeting of the MBT was held in October 2013 and the Magga Bhavaka Trust has now
                been wound up. The possibility of purchasing the freehold to secure the longer term future of the monastery is still being
                explored.
                ACTIVITIES
                    The development at Harnham Lake at the foot of the hill from the monastery continued during the year. The board
                walk, constructed to enable an easy circumnavigation of the lake, was completed together with the finishing touches to
                the three kutis and storage barn. This included privacy gates for the kutis and solar panels to provide electricity for the
                barn. A craftsman-built gazebo sponsored by the Burmese community, was erected to provide shelter for visitors, as well
                as an obelisk with an inlaid stone carving of the Buddha. There have been regular work days when community members
                and volunteers have worked together continuing the landscaping, cutting grass and planting more trees, shrubs and
                pond plants. The trustees continue to be inspired by the generous donations which have gone towards making this
                important and enhancing development possible
                    Other major work carried out during the year included repainting of the interior of Kusala House, the refurbishment of
                the abbot's kuti bathroom and a new larder immediately outside the kitchen. At Kusala House a hearing induction loop
                has been installed and the grey water recycling system is now operational. A stunning slate monastery sign that was
                sponsored, has made it easier for new visitors to find the monastery.
                THE MONASTERY COMMITTEE
                    This group of lay supporters and community members acts in the day-to-day management of the monastery and
                includes honorary members of the Thai and Sri Lankan communities. They are in effect a sub-committee to the Trust and
                see to such matters as car insurance, organizing festivals and retreats and liaising between the laity and the monastery
                residents. The group meets about every six weeks depending on the extent of activities. If you are interested in the work
                of the committee or have any questions or concerns please feel free to contact either the monastery directly or our Office
                Administrator, Penny Wakefield-Pearce by telephone on 01661881612 or email penny@ratanagiri.org.uk
                FINANCE
                   The Harnham Buddhist Monastery Trust, as a registered charity and as steward of the monastery's assets, works to
                ensure that the monastery finances are dealt with in accordance with the practices and values of the Theravada Buddhist
                tradition and in furtherance of the aims of the charity. The trustees feel it is important that supporters of the monastery
                have an idea about the general running costs incurred and a brief summary may be found below.
                   In 2013 the basic running costs for Harnham Monastery totalled £67767, or £5647 per month. This represents an
                increase of about £9000 over the previous year. An increase was to be expected with the extended facilties that we now
                have at the lake. We also had some additional expenses in order to bring the kitchen in to line with Health and Safety
                requirements and there were some extra costs for the sewage system this year. We have increased the hours worked by
                the Office Administrator, so that the Sangha do not need to be involved with too much administration.
                   The running of lay retreat accommodation at Kusala House cost an additional £7307 for the year, however many of
                the expenses of Kusala House, such as rates and insurance, are included in the monasteries running costs.
                    Taking the above into account, the running costs have been kept at a similar level to the previous year. Some

                TEACHING AND EVENTS
                   Luang Por Munindo continued his public teaching at the monastery, giving Dhamma talks on the first Saturday of each
                month and on various other occasions. He also continued to provide a 'Verse for Reflection', sent out twice a month to a
                global e-mailing list of monastery friends and supporters, which has more than 600 subscribers. The Forest Sangha
                Publications website is managed at Harnham on behalf of other associated monasteries.
                   Dhamma talks were offered on various other Saturdays by Ajahn Abhinando and Ajahn Punnyo and occasionally trom
                visiting Ajahns.
                   The monastery hosted 5 meditation retreats during the year led by Ajahn Karuniko, Ajahn Nyanarato and Ajahn
                Anando from associated monasteries as well as Ajahn Abhinando and Ajahn Punnyo.
                   Ajahn Abhinando also led a retreat in Spain and two weekend retreats in Edinburgh. Additionally he led Practice and
                Study days at the monastery which were all well attended.
                   Ajahn Abhinando and Ajahn Punnyo also made visits to meditation groups in Glasgow, Edinburgh, Carlisle and Leeds.
                Between them they have also offered Dhamma discussions for lay visitors on Saturdays and midweek whenever they
                were available.
                    Dr. Adrian Bint continued to conduct a monthly beginners' meditation class throughout the year and Kath Jones led
                the monthly lay discussion meetings.
                   Wesak in May and Kathina in October were the main festivals held at the monastery. The Wesak festival was again
               celebrated with a Saturday evening puja. It has been decided to keep this new format for the current year. Kathina was
                held on a Sunday afternoon as usual and attracted many visitors including several groups of Thai students from local
                universities.
                   The year ended with the annual New Year's Eve vigil which was well attended as usual.
