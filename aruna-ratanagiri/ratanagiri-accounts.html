What this means

Running costs: everything the Trust spends in a year to keep the monastery going, without new buildings and infrastructure (counted as something the Trust owns) and without wear on buildings (depreciation, not money paid out). Residents: the monks, novices and anagārikas living at Aruna Ratanagiri, on average; guests at Kusala House are not counted. Inflation: the UK consumer prices index. All amounts as paid at the time, unless the page says "in today's money".

      5. Notes and all figures

      • Where the figures come from. Every figure is read from the Trust's annual accounts as filed at Companies House. All of them are scans; every cost line was read by eye from the page and checked: each year's lines add up to the total spending printed in the accounts, and each year was compared with the next year's report, which repeats it.
      • Years. The Trust's year runs from January to December. The page starts in 2011: the Harnham Buddhist Monastery Trust took over from the Magga Bhavaka Trust in 2010, which brought in the buildings and money of the old trust (£1.0 million) and is not a normal year.
      • What the accounts cover. The whole monastery on the hill at Harnham, including Kusala House (the guest house for lay visitors), Mangala House and the lake land below the monastery. Part of the monastery, No. 2 Harnham Hall Cottages (reception room and kitchen), is rented: the rent is in "rent, rates and insurance".
      • ¹ The earlier trust. The Harnham Buddhist Monastery Trust took over the monastery from the Magga Bhavaka Trust (charity 326695, registered 1984), which is the same community under an earlier name. For 2011 and 2012 the Magga Bhavaka Trust still filed small accounts of its own (spending £8,821 and £4,614, income £8,444 and £2,782; Charity Commission register). They are added to those two years, under "office, staff and other", so the total spending and income of 2011 and 2012 are the Harnham Trust's accounts plus these. The earlier trust's accounts for 2013 and 2014 were never filed, and its older ones are not online.
      • Running costs are total spending in the accounts without depreciation (wear on buildings) and without small accounting losses on things sold or thrown away (£280 in 2023, £892 in 2024). New buildings and infrastructure are never in total spending: the accounts count them as things the Trust owns.
      • Repairs include work paid from gifts for a set purpose (restricted funds). They were high in 2013 and 2014 (about £37,000 and £40,000): the reports of those years describe re-roofing the Byre, repainting Kusala House and refurbishing the abbot's kuti bathroom. They are counted as running costs, as the accounts do.
      • Heat and light is the accounts' "light and heat"; the trustees' own summary calls it "utilities and firewood". It shows money paid, not energy used.
      • Kinds of cost. The accounts list about twenty lines; the page groups them: heat and light; repairs; rent, rates and insurance; welfare, household and food (bhikkhu welfare and Pavāraṇā, household, breakfast and kitchen); car and travel; teaching, books and offerings (events and retreats, publications, dāna, items of religious significance); office, staff and other (the part-time office staff, office, phone, accountant, legal, bank charges, sundry and miscellaneous). Much of the food is given by supporters and is not in the accounts.
      • Office, staff and other roughly doubled. Part of the rise is a line called "miscellaneous expenses", paid from gifts for a set purpose: £8,722 in 2021, £4,191 in 2022, £11,491 in 2023, £1,955 in 2024 and £4,471 in 2025. The accounts don't say what it was spent on. The office staff cost (one part-time post) rose from £4,608 in 2011 to £14,114 in 2025.
      • Line names changed over the years. "Postage" (to 2015) is called "office admin" from 2016; "lockdown expenses" (2020–2022) is called "kitchen expenses" from 2023. When a later report regroups a year (2015, 2023), the year's own report is used.
      • Residents are the monks, novices and anagārikas on the community lists (the Winter list in January and the Vassa list in July). Guests, retreatants and lay residents are not counted. Running costs are not divided by the number of residents: the costs also serve guests, retreatants and visitors.
      • Inflation. The UK consumer prices index (ONS, CPI), averaged over each calendar year. "In today's money" means in 2025 prices. Energy prices: the ONS index for household electricity, gas and other fuels.
